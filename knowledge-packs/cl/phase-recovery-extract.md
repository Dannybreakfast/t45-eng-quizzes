# 6. Recovery — Approach, Arrival, Tower & Landing

## Doc

Check in with approach on channel 13
Technique: When you switch to channel 13 switch aux to channel 5 so that you can hear if there will be any conflictions with other a/c on your way to WADDE for example.

In the jet at some point 10 minutes out from recovery you want to call base and tell them that you are 10 minutes out most of the time to facilitate hot pits/switching.

Moa rules state to exit at 9,000’ however since you are now back on your IFR flight plan under ATC control, they can clear you to exit at any altitude. If there is other traffic that may conflict in the area, ATC will contact them on the area freq and deconflict with them for you.
Proceed to WAADE and ensure you are set up for the approach. At WAADE and inbound, report commencing and begin your approach. When executing an approach ensure you have completed the “WARP” checks (Weather, Altimeter Setting, Runway in use, perform penetration checks). For a HI TACAN, set published penetration course in HSI and select CDI mode. At the IAF, intercept, and maintain the approach course as published. For a direct entry you do not need to do a lap of holding since this is a turn for alignment and not a HILO (its not bold). If you are coming in for a direct entry just commence the penetration descent and turn towards the penetration course once hitting WAADE to save fuel. At 250 KIAS, reduce power to idle and simultaneously lower the nose to about 5 degrees nose low and then extend the speed brakes and pitch for 10 degrees nose low so you don’t get slow. If you go idle boards while level at 250 and then try to pitch for 10 deg nose low you will get slow.

Technique for penetration descent Hi-TACAN 13R: If you retract boards at 8k instead of platform that works out well for fuel conservation to stay higher a little longer.
Anticipate reaching 5000 ft AGL on the arc. Allow the LAW to sound, report “Platform” and retract the speed brakes, if still extended. Shallow descent IAW the minute to live rule and reset LAW to the next altitude restriction as directed in your wing’s SOP. Just like how we lowered the nose before extending the speed brakes, start raising the nose towards 2 deg nose down before retracting the speed brakes so you don’t get super fast. 2 deg nose down at idle maintains 250 KIAS. Approaching 5,000 feet you can enter the next law setting and just don’t press enter yet because you need to let the law sound at 5,000. Then when the law sounds all you need to do is press enter and you can simultaneously retract the speed brakes to observe the minute to live rule.
“Platform, resetting LAW to 2800 ft”
At 5-7 nm prior to the FAF, slow to 200 KIAS. We will be at 3000 ft finishing up the arc. Use the turn onto the final approach course to slow from 250 KIAS to 200 KIAS. Go to idle at the lead radial and let the a/s bleed off. Configure the a/c for landing 3-5 nm prior to FAF (pretty much right after turning onto FAC and within 5 radials for the HI-TACAN 13R). When you put the gear down nudge power forward to about 1000-1200 pph to engage the approach idle stop (NATOPS says to do this somewhere). Hold attitude on the horizon and descend while you slow to on speed and do landing checks since the FAF altitude is coming up quick. Reset the law to about 10% below the next altitude before descending so it doesn’t go off.
Transitions (Clean to Dirty):
        Reduce power to idle and extend speed brakes if necessary. Slow to 200 KIAS and note level flight attitude on the ADI display. Select gear down and flaps to half (if required, flaps may be lowered, but not raised, in a turn). The increased lift and drag combination causes a “ballooning” effect that must be countered with firm forward stick force to maintain altitude and prevent climbing. Wait for stick pressure to dampen out before selecting full flaps, if desired. As stick forces settle down, trim nose up as the a/c decelerates. As a/s approaches 160 KIAS, increase power for 150 KIAS (1900 pph for half or 2500 pph for full) (Note: Don’t do this just go right to optimum AOA). Decelerate to optimum AOA (no sooner than 10 nm from runway, 30 deg of FAC) by reducing power, adding noseup trim to maintain level flight, and then adding the appropriate amount of power to achieve level flight.
Landing Checks:
“Below 200 kts, gear and __ flaps”
R/C: “Clear”

                Verify all position indicator lights. Gear handle light should be out.
2. Flaps/Slats - Full/Half/Up
                Verify with lights. Visually verify slats are down.
3. Hook - Up/down
                Verify with hook lever position and hook light.
4. Harness - As Required
                Probably unlocked unless you are taking a trap or practicing for, or doing actual carrier landings.
5. Speed Brakes - Extended/Retracted
                Verify with position indicator lights.
6. Anti-Skid - On/LT On
                Verify switch position and that light is on.

8. “Gear three down and locked, flaps set ____ (full/half/up), indicating____ (full/half/up), hook___(up/down), harness____(locked/unlocked), speed brakes_____(extended/retracted), anti-skid on, light on, fuel X.X, on-speed is XXX. Landing Checks Complete”
Descend to FAF altitude configured with speed brakes retracted and slow to on speed. Level off at FAF altitude using a good lead point and power at about 1800 pph to maintain level flight.
Optimum AOA Descent and Level Off:
        Once established in level flight with desired configuration at optimum AOA, extend speed brakes to begin descent at FAF. Apply forward stick pressure and/or trim to counter nose pitch and influence the nose down toward desired VSI. Initially, this will cause a fast indication. As stick pressure subsides, retrim noseup for optimum AOA and readjust power to maintain desired VSI (Max of 1000 fpm inside FAF).
        A good power setting for non-precision descent is 1500-1600 pph to achieve a descent rate of 800-1000 fpm. A good power setting for a precision descent is 1700 pph +/- 200 pph. This achieves a 650-700 fpm descent which is perfect for a 3 deg glideslope at half flaps.
Note: Extending boards without adding power will give approximately 500 fpm descent at optimum AOA. Lead corrections to AOA and attitude before they are needed so that corrections are on before the AOA reaches its peak or valley. For example, if fast on AOA and descent rate is good, you must lift the nose to onspeed and reduce power, then you must add power before the AOA could become slow to hold that descent rate. 3 part power corrections like in forms apply.
Approximately 100 feet prior to MDA (200 feet is more conservative and then ease down if needed), add about 300 pph and apply slight backstick pressure to level off while maintaining optimum AOA. If you are approaching MDA fast, better to put a bunch of power in and get fast to avoid busting MDA.
At the FAF, start the clock (not needed on the HI-TACAN for 13R but a good idea to do it anyways), extend speed brakes, report landing checklist complete on the ICS and make gear down call to ATC. Reset LAW before it goes off (Maybe have it ready right before you hit the FAF or just punch it in early when stabilized and approaching FAF). Forward stick pressure will be required to counter the pitch up due to speed brake extension. Technique: Leave power where it is and extend speed brakes while lowering the nose. Then pull power back to about 1500 pph for a non precision descent.
Ch 14 Pri: “Kingsville Tower, Blazer 201, FAF with gear”
“Blazer 201, winds 130 at 15, cleared for the option 13R”
“Cleared for the option 13R, Blazer 201”
Descend to the MDA at about 800-1000 fpm maintaining on speed and start looking for the runway. Monitor DME for the VDP and MAP. Approximately 100 feet prior to the MDA. add about 300 pph fuel flow and apply slight backstick pressure to level off at MDA.
Transitioning for Landing: With the field in sight and the a/c is in a position to safely land, transition to the final landing configuration (full flaps), and manage the energy state with speed brakes. When transitioning to full flaps, a nose down attitude is required to prevent ballooning. If no intent to execute a missed approach, reduce power to idle at touchdown. You can also just stay at half flaps for a touch and go but make sure if you do the descent rate is less than 600 fpm. Full stops must be done at full flaps.
Missed Approach Climb Out and Level Off:
        Simultaneously advance throttle to MRT, retract speed brakes, and rotate at optimum AOA, to approximately 12-15 deg nose up. Maintain this attitude on ADI display and rate of climb should increase toward 2,000 fpm. With two positive rates call for gear. At 125 KIAS minimum flaps may be called for and raised from full to half. With 140 KIAS min the flaps may be called for and raise from full or half to up (do not raise flaps in a turn). If a low level off altitude is required when remaining in the GCA pattern, you can reduce power below MRT to avoid a super high attitude when cleaning up which could cause you to blow through your altitude. As the a/c accelerates towards climb speed (200 KIAS if remaining in the approach pattern/GCA box, 250 KIAS otherwise), adjust backstick to maintain this a/s. Once established in a clean MRT climb (a 200 KIAS MRT climb will stabilize at approximately 4,000 fpm and 20 deg nose up; a 250 KIAS MRT climb will stabilize at approximately 6,000 fpm or greater and 18 deg nose up), lead level off by 1,000 ft, smoothly reducing power for desired a/s and allowing the nose to drop.
        Technique for missed approach or climbout into GCA box where you are staying at 200 knots: MRT boards, pause or two, then takeoff attitude (10-12 deg nose up), gear up flaps up then 15 deg nose up. With gear up and clean pull power back to 2200 and go to 10 deg nose up for the remainder of the climb.
If you have flown an instrument approach to the runway in use, the last person you talked to was a final controller, not Tower (they contacted Tower on your behalf and got your landing clearance for you). Therefore, after you have landed (for a full stop), contact Tower and give them a courtesy call to let them know you’re on the active.
Ch 3 Pri: “Kingsville Tower, Blazer 201, GCA rollout on the right” (as in 13R)
“Blazer 201, right when able, contact Ground”
“Roger, Blazer 201”
If you are already up BTN 3 upon landing you do not need to make the gca rollout on the right call. This is really only if you are up BTN 12 for example with a GCA controller.
After your full stop, taxi clear of the runway in use. Switch ground (ch 2) on pri and monitor. Complete After Landing Checklist at a stop.
