## Title Screen
Introduction to INAV and Voice Procedures (T-45 Goshawk graphic)

## Slide 1 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities
Ground Control: Taxi instructions to/from the duty; Obtains/relays IFR clearances if clearance delivery is not available; Provides weather information.
Clearance delivery: Has no control function; Used solely for the delivery of IFR clearances.

## Slide 2 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities
Tower: Controls runway traffic; Issues takeoff clearance; Controls airborne traffic in control area; Provides airport advisory information to arriving flight in absence of ATIS; Issues landing clearance.

## Slide 3 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities
Departure Control: Responsible for ensuring separation between departures; Sequences and separates IFR and participating VFR traffic; Provides radar service, including assistance for VFR traffic.

## Slide 4 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities
ARTCC: Central authority for issuing IFR clearances; Issues route assignment; Provides traffic separation; Issues NOTAMs; Provides current weather information as required.

## Slide 5 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities
Approach Control: Controls all IFR aircraft from ARTCC control to tower control; Provides radar service for IFR traffic separation and sequencing, and also assists VFR traffic; Provides arrival information to incoming traffic on initial contact.

## Slide 6 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities
FSS: Primary purpose services to civilian aircraft but may be utilized by military; Common frequencies 122.2 and 255.4; Receives and processes IFR/VFR flight plans; Relays communications between ARTCC and aircraft when communication difficulties arise; Assists lost aircraft; Solicits PIREPs.

## Slide 7 — Clearances (Normal Elements)
Aircraft identification; Clearance limit; Departure instructions or SID; Route of flight; Altitude assignment; Departure frequency; IFF code; Any holding instructions; Other information as required.

## Slide 8 — Clearances (readback)
Training command: Read back all clearances verbatim.
Real world: Must read back all runway assignment and hold short instructions; When clearance deviates from originally filed, pilot must read back to indicate acceptance; Not required to read back clearances (except items above) that remain the same as originally filed; If clearance is unacceptable, pilot may refuse and a new clearance will be issued; All readbacks preceded by aircraft identification/call sign.

## Slide 9 — Mandatory Voice Reports
FIH Section B (Position Reports): Handout; Holding — Always report entering or leaving with one exception: Military terminal, radar contact, instrument training; Level at new altitude — Need not report when level at new altitude unless in RVSM airspace (FL290-FL410).

## Slide 10 — Lost Communications
Squawk IFF code 7600 under all weather conditions; Attempt radio contact on other/previous frequencies; Guard frequencies are 243.0 and 121.5; ATC will attempt to communicate on Guard and any available frequencies of NAVAIDs.

## Slide 11 — Lost Communications (Primary objective)
To preclude extended IFR operation in the air traffic control system in VMC; To land as soon as practicable.

## Slide 12 — Lost Communications (VMC)
Maintain VMC and land as soon as practicable. Does not mean: land minutes short of destination; land at unsuitable airfield; land at unauthorized airfield. Observe VFR cruising altitudes if possible (East odd, west even +500). Observe control tower for light signals. Acknowledge: rocking wings (day); flash aircraft lights (night).

## Slide 13 — Lost Communications (IMC)
Continue flight — AVEFAME. Determine: correct route to fly; correct altitude to fly for each route segment; correct time to leave a clearance limit that either is or is not a fix from which an approach begins.

## Slide 14 — Lost Communications (Route priority AVEF)
A — route assigned in last clearance; V — if radar vectored, direct to fix/airway specified in clearance; E — route ATC advised may be expected in further clearance; F — route filed in flight plan.
WARNING: When on IFR flight plan and cleared to an intermediate point not on filed route and short of destination, do NOT accept without first obtaining expected further routing.

## Slide 15 — Lost Communications (Altitude — highest of AME)
A — altitude assigned in last ATC clearance; M — minimum altitude for IFR (MEA, MOCA, ESA, MSA, OROCA); E — altitude ATC advised may be expected in further clearance.

## Slide 16 — Leave clearance limit
Fix from which approach begins: commence descent/approach as close as possible to EFC if received, else as close as possible to ETA (T/O + ETE).
Fix from which approach does not begin: leave at EFC if received, else upon arrival over clearance limit.

## Slides 17–18 — In-Flight Emergency Communication Procedures
Approach magazine mishap story intro / crash photos (context slides).

## Slide 19 — In-Flight Emergency Communication Procedures
Set transponder to 7700. Transmit as many as possible: MAYDAY×3 (Distress) or PAN-PAN×3 (Urgency); Name of station addressed; Aircraft identification and type; Nature of distress or urgency; Weather; Pilot's intentions; Present position and heading (or last known position/time/heading if lost); Altitude; Fuel remaining; Number of people on board; Any other useful information.
