# Flashcards — NAV0102 Introduction to INAV and Voice Procedures

## 1
**Q:** What are Ground Control’s main responsibilities?
**A:** Taxi instructions to/from the duty runway; obtain/relay IFR clearances if Clearance Delivery is unavailable; provide weather information.

## 2
**Q:** Does Clearance Delivery have a control function?
**A:** No — it is used solely for the delivery of IFR clearances.

## 3
**Q:** What does the Tower control and issue?
**A:** Controls runway traffic and airborne traffic in the control area; issues takeoff and landing clearances; provides airport advisory info to arriving flights if ATIS is absent.

## 4
**Q:** What does Departure Control do?
**A:** Ensures separation between departures; sequences/separates IFR and participating VFR; provides radar service including VFR assistance.

## 5
**Q:** What is ARTCC’s role regarding IFR clearances?
**A:** Central authority for issuing IFR clearances; also issues route assignments, provides traffic separation, issues NOTAMs, and provides current weather as required.

## 6
**Q:** What does Approach Control handle?
**A:** Controls all IFR aircraft from ARTCC to tower; radar separation/sequencing for IFR and assists VFR; provides arrival information on initial contact.

## 7
**Q:** FSS common frequencies and primary purpose?
**A:** Primarily civilian services (military may use); common freqs 122.2 and 255.4. Also flight plans, ARTCC relay, lost-aircraft assist, PIREPs.

## 8
**Q:** List the normal elements of an IFR clearance.
**A:** Aircraft ID; clearance limit; departure instructions or SID; route; altitude; departure frequency; IFF code; any holding instructions; other info as required.

## 9
**Q:** Training-command vs real-world clearance readback?
**A:** Training: read back all clearances verbatim. Real world: must read back runway assignment and hold-short instructions; read back deviations from filed to show acceptance; may refuse unacceptable clearances; precede readbacks with call sign.

## 10
**Q:** When must you report entering or leaving holding?
**A:** Always, except: military terminal, radar contact, instrument training.

## 11
**Q:** When must you report leveling at a new altitude?
**A:** Need not report unless in RVSM airspace (FL290–FL410).

## 12
**Q:** Lost communications — what IFF/transponder code and Guard freqs?
**A:** Squawk 7600 under all weather conditions. Guard: 243.0 and 121.5. Also try other/previous frequencies; ATC will use Guard and NAVAID freqs.

## 13
**Q:** Primary objective of lost-communications procedures?
**A:** Preclude extended IFR operation in the ATC system in VMC; land as soon as practicable.

## 14
**Q:** VMC lost-comms actions and light-signal acknowledgments?
**A:** Maintain VMC and land ASAP (not at unsuitable/unauthorized fields or minutes short of dest). Use VFR cruising altitudes if possible (E odd / W even+500). Rock wings (day) or flash lights (night) to acknowledge tower light signals.

## 15
**Q:** What does AVEFAME mean for IMC lost communications?
**A:** Continue flight using AVEFAME: determine correct route (AVEF), altitude (AME — highest), and time to leave the clearance limit.

## 16
**Q:** Lost-comms route priority (AVEF)?
**A:** A = Assigned in last clearance; V = if Vectored, direct to fix/airway in clearance; E = Expected further routing; F = Filed flight-plan route.

## 17
**Q:** WARNING about clearance to an intermediate point off your filed route?
**A:** Do not accept a clearance to an intermediate point not on your filed route and short of destination without first obtaining expected further routing.

## 18
**Q:** Lost-comms altitude — fly the highest of which (AME)?
**A:** A = altitude Assigned in last clearance; M = Minimum IFR altitude (MEA, MOCA, ESA, MSA, OROCA); E = Expected altitude from ATC.

## 19
**Q:** When do you leave a clearance limit that IS a fix from which an approach begins?
**A:** Commence descent/approach as close as possible to EFC if received; otherwise as close as possible to ETA (T/O + ETE).

## 20
**Q:** When do you leave a clearance limit that is NOT a fix from which an approach begins?
**A:** Leave at EFC if received; otherwise upon arrival over the clearance limit.

## 21
**Q:** In-flight emergency — transponder code and distress vs urgency call?
**A:** Squawk 7700. Distress: MAYDAY (slide: “MAY, MAY, MAYDAY”); Urgency: PAN-PAN ×3. Then station, ID/type, nature, weather, intentions, position/heading, altitude, fuel, souls on board, other useful info.

## 22
**Q:** What mnemonic covers IMC lost-comms route + altitude selection?
**A:** AVEF (route) + AME (altitude) → AVEFAME; fly highest of A/M/E for each route segment.
