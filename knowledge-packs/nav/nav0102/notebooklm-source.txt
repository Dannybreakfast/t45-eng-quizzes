# Summary — NAV0102 Introduction to INAV and Voice Procedures

**Source:** tsharp.navsea e-learning screen recording (`source.mp4`, ~28s, silent)  
**Course title:** Introduction to INAV and Voice Procedures  
**Content type:** Slide OCR / watchVideo visual transcription (19 slides). No usable audio — content is visual only.

## Short summary
Overview of **ATC facility responsibilities** (Ground, Clearance Delivery, Tower, Departure, ARTCC, Approach, FSS), **IFR clearance elements and readback rules** (training vs real-world), **mandatory voice reports** (holding; level-off in RVSM), **lost communications** (squawk 7600, Guard, VMC land ASAP, IMC **AVEFAME** route/altitude/leave-clearance-limit), and **in-flight emergency communications** (squawk 7700; MAYDAY / PAN-PAN elements).

## Key takeaways
1. **Ground Control:** Taxi to/from duty runway; can obtain/relay IFR clearances if Clearance Delivery unavailable; weather info.
2. **Clearance Delivery:** No control function — solely delivers IFR clearances.
3. **Tower:** Runway traffic, takeoff/landing clearances, airborne traffic in control area; airport advisories if no ATIS.
4. **Departure Control:** Separates departures; sequences IFR and participating VFR; radar service / VFR assistance.
5. **ARTCC:** Central IFR clearance authority; routes, separation, NOTAMs, weather as required.
6. **Approach Control:** IFR from ARTCC handoff to tower; radar separation/sequencing; assists VFR; arrival info on initial contact.
7. **FSS:** Primarily civilian (military may use); freqs **122.2 / 255.4**; flight plans; ARTCC relay; lost aircraft; PIREPs.
8. **Clearance elements:** ID, limit, SID/departure instructions, route, altitude, departure freq, IFF, holding, other as required.
9. **Readback:** Training — all clearances verbatim. Real world — must read back runway assignment & hold-short; deviations from filed; refuse unacceptable clearances; precede with call sign.
10. **Mandatory reports (FIH §B):** Report entering/leaving holding except military terminal + radar contact + instrument training; report level at new altitude in **RVSM (FL290–FL410)**.
11. **Lost comms initial:** Squawk **7600**; try other freqs; Guard **243.0 / 121.5**; ATC uses Guard and NAVAID freqs.
12. **Lost-comms objective:** Avoid extended IFR in the ATC system in VMC; land as soon as practicable (not “any unsuitable/unauthorized field”).
13. **VMC lost comms:** Maintain VMC; VFR altitudes (E odd / W even+500); watch tower light signals; rock wings (day) / flash lights (night).
14. **IMC lost comms — AVEFAME:** Route **AVEF** (Assigned → Vectored → Expected → Filed); altitude highest of **AME** (Assigned / Minimum IFR / Expected); leave clearance limit per EFC or ETA (T/O+ETE) / arrival rules.
15. **WARNING:** Do not accept clearance to an intermediate point off filed route short of destination without expected further routing.
16. **Emergency:** Squawk **7700**; MAYDAY (distress) or PAN-PAN (urgency) plus station, ID/type, nature, weather, intentions, position/heading, altitude, fuel, souls on board, other useful info.

# Flashcards — NAV0102 Introduction to INAV and Voice Procedures

## 1
**Q:** What are Ground Control’s main responsibilities?
**A:** Taxi instructions to/from the duty runway; obtain/relay IFR clearances if Clearance Delivery is unavailable; provide weather information.

## 2
**Q:** Does Clearance Delivery have a control function?
**A:** No — it is used solely for the delivery of IFR clearances.

## 3
**Q:** What does the Tower control and issue?
**A:** Controls runway traffic and airborne traffic in the control area; issues takeoff and landing clearances; provides airport advisory info to arriving flights if ATIS is absent.

## 4
**Q:** What does Departure Control do?
**A:** Ensures separation between departures; sequences/separates IFR and participating VFR; provides radar service including VFR assistance.

## 5
**Q:** What is ARTCC’s role regarding IFR clearances?
**A:** Central authority for issuing IFR clearances; also issues route assignments, provides traffic separation, issues NOTAMs, and provides current weather as required.

## 6
**Q:** What does Approach Control handle?
**A:** Controls all IFR aircraft from ARTCC to tower; radar separation/sequencing for IFR and assists VFR; provides arrival information on initial contact.

## 7
**Q:** FSS common frequencies and primary purpose?
**A:** Primarily civilian services (military may use); common freqs 122.2 and 255.4. Also flight plans, ARTCC relay, lost-aircraft assist, PIREPs.

## 8
**Q:** List the normal elements of an IFR clearance.
**A:** Aircraft ID; clearance limit; departure instructions or SID; route; altitude; departure frequency; IFF code; any holding instructions; other info as required.

## 9
**Q:** Training-command vs real-world clearance readback?
**A:** Training: read back all clearances verbatim. Real world: must read back runway assignment and hold-short instructions; read back deviations from filed to show acceptance; may refuse unacceptable clearances; precede readbacks with call sign.

## 10
**Q:** When must you report entering or leaving holding?
**A:** Always, except: military terminal, radar contact, instrument training.

## 11
**Q:** When must you report leveling at a new altitude?
**A:** Need not report unless in RVSM airspace (FL290–FL410).

## 12
**Q:** Lost communications — what IFF/transponder code and Guard freqs?
**A:** Squawk 7600 under all weather conditions. Guard: 243.0 and 121.5. Also try other/previous frequencies; ATC will use Guard and NAVAID freqs.

## 13
**Q:** Primary objective of lost-communications procedures?
**A:** Preclude extended IFR operation in the ATC system in VMC; land as soon as practicable.

## 14
**Q:** VMC lost-comms actions and light-signal acknowledgments?
**A:** Maintain VMC and land ASAP (not at unsuitable/unauthorized fields or minutes short of dest). Use VFR cruising altitudes if possible (E odd / W even+500). Rock wings (day) or flash lights (night) to acknowledge tower light signals.

## 15
**Q:** What does AVEFAME mean for IMC lost communications?
**A:** Continue flight using AVEFAME: determine correct route (AVEF), altitude (AME — highest), and time to leave the clearance limit.

## 16
**Q:** Lost-comms route priority (AVEF)?
**A:** A = Assigned in last clearance; V = if Vectored, direct to fix/airway in clearance; E = Expected further routing; F = Filed flight-plan route.

## 17
**Q:** WARNING about clearance to an intermediate point off your filed route?
**A:** Do not accept a clearance to an intermediate point not on your filed route and short of destination without first obtaining expected further routing.

## 18
**Q:** Lost-comms altitude — fly the highest of which (AME)?
**A:** A = altitude Assigned in last clearance; M = Minimum IFR altitude (MEA, MOCA, ESA, MSA, OROCA); E = Expected altitude from ATC.

## 19
**Q:** When do you leave a clearance limit that IS a fix from which an approach begins?
**A:** Commence descent/approach as close as possible to EFC if received; otherwise as close as possible to ETA (T/O + ETE).

## 20
**Q:** When do you leave a clearance limit that is NOT a fix from which an approach begins?
**A:** Leave at EFC if received; otherwise upon arrival over the clearance limit.

## 21
**Q:** In-flight emergency — transponder code and distress vs urgency call?
**A:** Squawk 7700. Distress: MAYDAY (slide: “MAY, MAY, MAYDAY”); Urgency: PAN-PAN ×3. Then station, ID/type, nature, weather, intentions, position/heading, altitude, fuel, souls on board, other useful info.

## 22
**Q:** What mnemonic covers IMC lost-comms route + altitude selection?
**A:** AVEF (route) + AME (altitude) → AVEFAME; fly highest of A/M/E for each route segment.

## Slide 4 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 4 of 19)

**Air Route Traffic Control Center (ARTCC)**
- Central authority for issuing IFR clearances
- Issues route assignment
- Provides traffic separation
- Issues NOTAMs
- Provides current weather information as required

---

## Slide 5 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 5 of 19)

**Approach Control**
- Controls all IFR aircraft from ARTCC control to tower control
- Provides radar service for IFR traffic separation and sequencing, and also assists VFR traffic
- Provides arrival information to incoming traffic on initial contact

---

## Slide 6 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 6 of 19)

**Flight Service Station (FSS)**
- Primary purpose is to provide services to civilian aircraft but may be utilized by military
- Common frequencies are 122.2 and 255.4
- Receives and processes IFR/VFR flight plans
- Relays communications between ARTCC and aircraft when communication difficulties arise
- Assists lost aircraft
- Solicits PIREPs

---

## Slide 7 — Clearances — Normal Elements (Page 7 of 19)

**Normal Elements**
- Aircraft identification
- Clearance limit
- Departure instructions or SID
- Route of flight
- Altitude assignment
- Departure frequency
- IFF code
- Any holding instructions
- Other information as required

---

## Slide 8 — Clearances — Readback (Page 8 of 19)

**Clearance readback**

*Training command*
- Read back all clearances verbatim.

*Real world*
- Must read back all runway assignment and hold short instructions
- When clearance deviates from originally filed, pilot must read back to indicate acceptance
- Not required to read back clearances (except for items above) that remain the same as originally filed
- If clearance is unacceptable, pilot has the option to refuse, and a new clearance will be issued
- All readbacks should be preceded by aircraft identification/call sign

---

## Slide 9 — Mandatory Voice Reports (Page 9 of 19)

**FIH, Section B (Position Reports)**
- Handout
- Holding — Always report entering or leaving with one exception: Military terminal, radar contact, instrument training
- Level at new altitude — Need not report when level at new altitude unless in RVSM airspace (FL290–FL410)

---

## Slide 10 — Lost Communications (Page 10 of 19)

- Squawk IFF code 7600 under all weather conditions
- Attempt radio contact on other/previous frequencies
- Guard frequencies are 243.0 and 121.5
- ATC will attempt to communicate on Guard and any available frequencies of NAVAIDs

---

## Slide 11 — Lost Communications — Primary objective (Page 11 of 19)

**Primary objective**
- To preclude extended IFR operation in the air traffic control system in VMC
- To land as soon as practicable

---

## Slide 12 — Lost Communications — VMC (Page 12 of 19)

**VMC conditions**
- Maintain VMC conditions and land as soon as practicable
- Does **not** mean: land minutes short of destination; land at an unsuitable airfield; land at an unauthorized airfield
- Observe VFR cruising altitudes, if possible (East is odd, west is even plus 500)
- Observe control tower for light signals
- Acknowledge by: rocking wings during daylight; flash aircraft lights during night time

---

## Slide 13 — Lost Communications — IMC (Page 13 of 19)

**IMC conditions**
- Continue flight — **AVEFAME**
- Determine:
  - Correct route to fly
  - Correct altitude to fly for each route segment
  - Correct time to leave a clearance limit that either is or is not a fix from which an approach begins

---

## Slide 14 — Lost Communications — Route priority AVEF (Page 14 of 19)

**Route (ranking by priority)**
- **A** — By route assigned in last clearance received
- **V** — If being radar vectored, by the direct route from the point of radio failure to the fix or airway specified in clearance
- **E** — In the absence of an assigned route, by the route ATC has advised may be expected in a further clearance
- **F** — In the absence of an assigned or expected route, by the route filed in the flight plan

**WARNING:** When on an IFR flight plan and cleared to an intermediate point which is not on your filed route and short of your destination, you should not accept the clearance without first obtaining your expected further routing.

---

## Slide 15 — Lost Communications — Altitude (highest of AME) (Page 15 of 19)

**Altitude** — At the highest of the following altitudes for the route segment being flown:
- **A** — The altitude assigned in the last ATC clearance received
- **M** — The minimum altitude for IFR operations (MEA, MOCA, ESA, MSA, OROCA)
- **E** — The altitude ATC has advised may be expected in a further clearance

---

## Slide 16 — Leave clearance limit (Page 16 of 19)

**Leave clearance limit**

*Fix from which an approach begins*
- Commence descent and approach as close as possible to the EFC if one has been received, or if one has not been received, as close as possible to the ETA (T/O + ETE)

*Fix from which an approach does not begin*
- Leave at the EFC if one has been received, or if one has not been received, upon arrival over the clearance limit

## Slide 19 — In-Flight Emergency Communication Procedures (Page 19 of 19)

- Set transponder to **7700**
- Transmit as many as possible of the following elements:
  - MAY, MAY, MAYDAY (Distress) *[slide wording; standard phraseology is MAYDAY three times]*
  - PAN-PAN, PAN-PAN, PAN-PAN (Urgency)
  - Name of station addressed
  - Aircraft identification and type
  - Nature of distress or urgency
  - Weather
  - Pilot's intentions
  - Present position and heading or if lost, last known position, time, heading since that position
  - Altitude
  - Fuel remaining
  - Number of people on board
  - Any other useful information
