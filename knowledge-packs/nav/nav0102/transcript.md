## Slide 1 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 1 of 19)

**Ground Control**
- Taxi instructions to/from the duty [runway — slide text truncates at "duty"]
- Obtains/relays IFR clearances if clearance delivery is not available
- Provides weather information

**Clearance delivery**
- Has no control function
- Used solely for the delivery of IFR clearances

---

## Slide 2 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 2 of 19)

**Tower**
- Controls runway traffic
- Issues takeoff clearance
- Controls airborne traffic in control area
- Provides airport advisory information to arriving flight in absence of ATIS
- Issues landing clearance

---

## Slide 3 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 3 of 19)

**Departure Control**
- Responsible for ensuring separation between departures
- Sequences and separates IFR and participating VFR traffic
- Provides radar service, including assistance for VFR traffic

---

## Slide 4 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 4 of 19)

**Air Route Traffic Control Center (ARTCC)**
- Central authority for issuing IFR clearances
- Issues route assignment
- Provides traffic separation
- Issues NOTAMs
- Provides current weather information as required

---

## Slide 5 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 5 of 19)

**Approach Control**
- Controls all IFR aircraft from ARTCC control to tower control
- Provides radar service for IFR traffic separation and sequencing, and also assists VFR traffic
- Provides arrival information to incoming traffic on initial contact

---

## Slide 6 — Responsibilities of ATC Facilities (Page 6 of 19)

**Flight Service Station (FSS)**
- Primary purpose is to provide services to civilian aircraft but may be utilized by military
- Common frequencies are 122.2 and 255.4
- Receives and processes IFR/VFR flight plans
- Relays communications between ARTCC and aircraft when communication difficulties arise
- Assists lost aircraft
- Solicits PIREPs

---

## Slide 7 — Clearances — Normal Elements (Page 7 of 19)

**Normal Elements**
- Aircraft identification
- Clearance limit
- Departure instructions or SID
- Route of flight
- Altitude assignment
- Departure frequency
- IFF code
- Any holding instructions
- Other information as required

---

## Slide 8 — Clearances — Readback (Page 8 of 19)

**Clearance readback**

*Training command*
- Read back all clearances verbatim.

*Real world*
- Must read back all runway assignment and hold short instructions
- When clearance deviates from originally filed, pilot must read back to indicate acceptance
- Not required to read back clearances (except for items above) that remain the same as originally filed
- If clearance is unacceptable, pilot has the option to refuse, and a new clearance will be issued
- All readbacks should be preceded by aircraft identification/call sign

---

## Slide 9 — Mandatory Voice Reports (Page 9 of 19)

**FIH, Section B (Position Reports)**
- Handout
- Holding — Always report entering or leaving with one exception: Military terminal, radar contact, instrument training
- Level at new altitude — Need not report when level at new altitude unless in RVSM airspace (FL290–FL410)

---

## Slide 10 — Lost Communications (Page 10 of 19)

- Squawk IFF code 7600 under all weather conditions
- Attempt radio contact on other/previous frequencies
- Guard frequencies are 243.0 and 121.5
- ATC will attempt to communicate on Guard and any available frequencies of NAVAIDs

---

## Slide 11 — Lost Communications — Primary objective (Page 11 of 19)

**Primary objective**
- To preclude extended IFR operation in the air traffic control system in VMC
- To land as soon as practicable

---

## Slide 12 — Lost Communications — VMC (Page 12 of 19)

**VMC conditions**
- Maintain VMC conditions and land as soon as practicable
- Does **not** mean: land minutes short of destination; land at an unsuitable airfield; land at an unauthorized airfield
- Observe VFR cruising altitudes, if possible (East is odd, west is even plus 500)
- Observe control tower for light signals
- Acknowledge by: rocking wings during daylight; flash aircraft lights during night time

---

## Slide 13 — Lost Communications — IMC (Page 13 of 19)

**IMC conditions**
- Continue flight — **AVEFAME**
- Determine:
  - Correct route to fly
  - Correct altitude to fly for each route segment
  - Correct time to leave a clearance limit that either is or is not a fix from which an approach begins

---

## Slide 14 — Lost Communications — Route priority AVEF (Page 14 of 19)

**Route (ranking by priority)**
- **A** — By route assigned in last clearance received
- **V** — If being radar vectored, by the direct route from the point of radio failure to the fix or airway specified in clearance
- **E** — In the absence of an assigned route, by the route ATC has advised may be expected in a further clearance
- **F** — In the absence of an assigned or expected route, by the route filed in the flight plan

**WARNING:** When on an IFR flight plan and cleared to an intermediate point which is not on your filed route and short of your destination, you should not accept the clearance without first obtaining your expected further routing.

---

## Slide 15 — Lost Communications — Altitude (highest of AME) (Page 15 of 19)

**Altitude** — At the highest of the following altitudes for the route segment being flown:
- **A** — The altitude assigned in the last ATC clearance received
- **M** — The minimum altitude for IFR operations (MEA, MOCA, ESA, MSA, OROCA)
- **E** — The altitude ATC has advised may be expected in a further clearance

---

## Slide 16 — Leave clearance limit (Page 16 of 19)

**Leave clearance limit**

*Fix from which an approach begins*
- Commence descent and approach as close as possible to the EFC if one has been received, or if one has not been received, as close as possible to the ETA (T/O + ETE)

*Fix from which an approach does not begin*
- Leave at the EFC if one has been received, or if one has not been received, upon arrival over the clearance limit

## Slide 19 — In-Flight Emergency Communication Procedures (Page 19 of 19)

- Set transponder to **7700**
- Transmit as many as possible of the following elements:
  - MAY, MAY, MAYDAY (Distress) *[slide wording; standard phraseology is MAYDAY three times]*
  - PAN-PAN, PAN-PAN, PAN-PAN (Urgency)
  - Name of station addressed
  - Aircraft identification and type
  - Nature of distress or urgency
  - Weather
  - Pilot's intentions
  - Present position and heading or if lost, last known position, time, heading since that position
  - Altitude
  - Fuel remaining
  - Number of people on board
  - Any other useful information
