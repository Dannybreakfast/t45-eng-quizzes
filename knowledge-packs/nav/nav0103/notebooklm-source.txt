# Summary — NAV0103 Departure and Terminal Procedures

**Source:** tsharp.navsea e-learning screen recording (`source.mp4`, ~148s, silent)  
**Course title:** Departure and Terminal Procedures  
**Content type:** Slide OCR / cleaned screen-content (148 frames) + selective chart re-OCR. No usable audio — content is visual only.

## Short summary
Covers **airspeed restrictions** (FAR 91 / Class B–D shelves & under-B / holding Navy–USAF–civilian), **CNAF/T-45 fuel reserve**, **takeoff weather criteria** (Special vs Standard Instrument Card; formation), **IFR departure types** (Diverse, ODP, Pilot/Vector SID with DD-1801 examples, Radar Departure & verbal ATC, VCOA, VFR Climb on Course), **STARs** (LEMIG ONE Cotulla example; descend via; No STAR), **instrument approaches** (SFA, single-pilot mins/HAT adjust with KNQI S-PAR math, commence/continue/practice rules, formation, visual vs contact, circling lost-visual, IAF altitude, missed/NORDO alternate altitudes, VDP/VDA & dive-and-drive), **VOR MON** (+ FLIP Low Altitude legend symbology), **closing flight plans**, and **logbook** awareness.

## Key takeaways
1. **250 KIAS** max below 10,000 ft MSL (FAR 91); DOD exemptions in restricted/MOA/exercises/military training routes/ND mission when recommended maneuvering speed is greater.
2. **Class B** 250 KIAS; **Class C/D** 200 KIAS within 4 nm / surface–2500 AGL (slide notes Class C shelves ~10 nm/1200 and 20 nm/4000); **200 KIAS** under Class B floors / VFR corridors through Class B.
3. **Holding:** max endurance (T-45 SOP **200 KIAS**); Navy max **230**; USAF **310**; civilian 200 / 230 / 265 by altitude band (0–6000 / to 14,000 / above 14,000).
4. **Fuel:** CNAF M-3710.7 → greater of 10% planned or 20 min max endurance at 10,000 MSL; **T-45 always 500 lb IFR** (TW-1/TW-2 SOP).
5. **Special Instrument Card:** no ceiling/vis mins; judgment/urgency; reqs 5 yrs / 2000 hrs / 100 actual IFR hrs; mins may be reduced.
6. **Standard Instrument Card:** takeoff wx from any compatible approach (TAC/VOR/ILS/LOC/PAR/ASR); NPA not less than **300-1**; PA not less than **200-1/2**.
7. **Formation takeoff wx:** ≥ circling mins for runway in use, or **1000-3** if no circling.
8. **Departure types (5+1):** Diverse; ODP; SID (Pilot NAV / Vector); Specific ATC (Radar + verbal non-radar); VCOA; + VFR Climb on Course.
9. **Diverse departure:** ≥1 IAP; no OIS **40:1** penetrations; runway hdg to **400 ft** AFE; **200 ft/nm** min climb; implied when “cleared as filed” with no climb gradient / “Terrible T” / further instructions (front of DOD plates).
10. **ODP:** when obstacles penetrate 40:1; **default** if no vectors/SID; one per runway; non-standard wx mins **do not apply** to USN/USMC; not advisable if they require see-and-avoid.
11. **SID:** primary = IMC obstacle clearance; secondary = efficiency/comms; not mandatory under CNAF but encouraged. May have min climb rates or higher takeoff wx (e.g. 300-1 vs PAR/ILS 200-1/2). Graphics at **rear** of approach plates.
12. **Pilot NAV vs Vector SID:** NQI3 CRP (Kingsville Three / CRP transition); CHA7 CHA (Chattanooga Seven VECTOR). Example route: `CHA7 CHA DCT RMG DCT MGM J39 CEW DCT INBRD DCT JAYDI DCT NPA`.
13. **SID cancel/altitude:** cancel unless “expect to resume SID”; reinstate needs remaining routing + restated altitude restrictions; restated altitude → climb immediately unless restrictions remain; **still fly ground track**.
14. **Radar departure:** no SID / direct to first point; file DCT + fix; Remarks **REQUEST RADAR DEPARTURE**. Specific ATC also includes verbal non-radar guidance.
15. **VCOA:** visual climb over airport/NAVAID when gradients/terrain preclude conventional DP; no identifier — Remarks intent to use VCOA (front of DOD plates).
16. **VFR Climb on Course:** stay VFR below Class A until IFR pickup; Remarks **REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE**; sample call ROKT 21 over Seminole 10,500 to Navy Pensacola.
17. **STAR:** maintain last altitude until authorized; **DESCEND VIA** = lateral+vertical per procedure; decline via Remarks (preferred) or “No STAR.”
18. **LEMIG ONE (Cotulla):** COT R-037 → LEMIG → SAT R-175 → ELKAY → expect vectors; turbojet expect **cross LEMIG at 10,000′**; SAT APP CON **125.7 / 381.4**.
19. **SFA:** single UHF to touchdown for single-piloted military turbojets night/IMC; CNAF SHALL provide max extent; minimize freq/code changes below **2500 AGL** if no SFA.
20. **Single-pilot mins:** absolute **200 HAT / ½ SM (2400 RVR)**; T-45 **200-1/2 or published, whichever higher**; raise DA by (200 − published HAT).
21. **KNQI example:** published DA **150** / HAT **100** → single-piloted DA **250** (S-PAR 13R 150-1/2 / HAT 100 highlighted).
22. **Commence rule:** no start at dest/alt if below mins; may continue if mins go down after start; practice to mins only at enroute fields with **no intent** to land at filed dest/alt.
23. **Formation approaches:** >2 a/c IMC approaches not authorized (IMC→VMC penetration OK); 2-ship ≥ circling or **1000-3**.
24. **Visual approach:** IFR procedure in VMC; 1000&3; field or traffic in sight; **no MAP**; does not cancel FP; pilot owns interval/wake if following. Cloud clearances N/A unless ops specs (500/1000/2000).
25. **Contact:** pilot-only request; **1 SM** vis; **is** an IAP with MAP; “at or below” ≥1000 below IFR traffic and not below MSA.
26. **Circling miss:** climb; turn toward landing runway; then to published MAP course. **IAF:** hold last alt until established on published segment after approach clearance (cross IAF → shortest turn → FAF).
27. **Missed:** advise ATC “executing missed approach.” **NORDO to alternate:** **7600**; published MAP; proceed as filed; DRAFT = route not altitude; alt options MSA 25 nm / ESA if <200 nm / **18,000 (≥29.92)** or **FL190 (<29.92)**.
28. **VDP:** advisory point for normal **3° (~300 ft/NM)** from MDA; contrast dive-and-drive vs constant-angle through VDP. **VDA** = FAF-to-threshold angle at TCH.
29. **VOR MON:** GPS/GNSS backup; CONUS coverage ≥**5000 AGL**; proceed to MON airport for ILS/VOR without GPS/DME/ADF/surveillance; rarely >**100 NM**. FLIP Low Altitude legend: BLUE/GREEN = IAP/radar mins; BROWN = none; dedicated **Military MON** airport symbol.
30. **Close FP:** verbal with Tower/Base Ops at military; FSS (any means) at civilian. **Logbook:** track medical, approaches, IFR/total time, flight types; verify entries (“Cover Your Six”).

# Flashcards — NAV0103 Departure and Terminal Procedures

## 1
**Q:** What is the FAR 91 maximum airspeed below 10,000 ft MSL, and why?
**A:** 250 KIAS maximum — to reduce midair collision hazards at low altitudes. Level off above 10,000 and slow before descending lower.

## 2
**Q:** Name the six DOD exemptions to the 250 KIAS below 10,000 rule.
**A:** Within restricted areas; within MOAs; large-scale exercises or short-term special mission; military IFR/VFR training routes; when recommended safe maneuvering airspeed is greater; National Defense Mission.

## 3
**Q:** Class B airspeed limit?
**A:** 250 KIAS maximum.

## 4
**Q:** Class C airspeed limit near the primary airport?
**A:** 200 KIAS within 4 nm of the primary airport, from the surface to 2500 ft AGL.

## 5
**Q:** What Class C footprint dimensions appear as a note on the airspeed slide?
**A:** 10 nm / 1200 ft AGL and 20 nm / 4000 ft AGL (as printed under the Class C bullet).

## 6
**Q:** Class D airspeed limit near the primary airport?
**A:** 200 KIAS within 4 nm of the primary airport, from the surface to 2500 ft AGL; individual footprint, typically to 2500 ft AGL.

## 7
**Q:** Where else is 200 KIAS the maximum besides Class C/D core?
**A:** Under floors of Class B airspace, and in a VFR corridor transiting Class B airspace.

## 8
**Q:** At what airspeed should you hold, and what is T-45 SOP?
**A:** Hold at MAXIMUM ENDURANCE airspeed; T-45 SOP is 200 KIAS.

## 9
**Q:** Maximum holding airspeed at Navy airfields?
**A:** 230 KIAS at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted.

## 10
**Q:** Maximum holding airspeed at USAF airfields?
**A:** 310 KIAS at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted.

## 11
**Q:** Civilian maximum holding airspeeds by altitude band?
**A:** 200 KIAS from 0–6000 ft MSL; 230 KIAS above 6000 to/including 14,000 ft MSL; 265 KIAS above 14,000 ft MSL.

## 12
**Q:** CNAF M-3710.7 fuel reserve requirement?
**A:** Greater of 10% of planned requirements or 20 minutes of flight at maximum endurance at 10,000 ft MSL.

## 13
**Q:** T-45 IFR fuel reserve per TW-1/TW-2 joint SOP?
**A:** ALWAYS 500 pounds IFR.

## 14
**Q:** Special Instrument Card takeoff weather rule?
**A:** No ceiling or visibility minimums apply; takeoff depends on pilot judgment and urgency of the flight. Minimums may be reduced.

## 15
**Q:** Special Instrument Card qualification requirements?
**A:** 5 years flight experience; 2000 hours flight time; 100 hours actual instrument time.

## 16
**Q:** What approaches may Standard Instrument Card takeoff weather be based on?
**A:** ANY approach compatible with installed and operable equipment: TAC/VOR/ILS/LOC/PAR/ASR.

## 17
**Q:** Standard Instrument Card non-precision takeoff weather floor?
**A:** Published minimums but not less than 300-1.

## 18
**Q:** Standard Instrument Card precision takeoff weather floor?
**A:** Published minimums but not less than 200-1/2.

## 19
**Q:** Formation takeoff weather criteria?
**A:** Equal or above published circling minimums for the runway in use; if circling not authorized/exists, need VFR 1000-3.

## 20
**Q:** List the IFR departure types (5+1).
**A:** Diverse; Obstacle DP (ODP); SID/DP (Pilot NAV and Vector); Specific ATC (Radar Departure and verbal non-radar instructions); VCOA; (+1) VFR Climb on Course.

## 21
**Q:** Diverse departure prerequisites regarding IAP and OIS?
**A:** Airfield has at least one IAP, and no obstacles penetrate the 40:1 Obstacle Identification Surface (OIS).

## 22
**Q:** Diverse departure heading and climb gradient rules?
**A:** Fly runway heading until 400 ft above field elevation before any turns; maintain minimum climb 200 ft/nm (unless higher published) to a minimum IFR altitude (MEA, MSA, etc.).

## 23
**Q:** When does ATC expect a diverse departure?
**A:** ATC will not specifically clear you. If “Cleared as filed,” no published min climb gradient or “Terrible T” for the departure runway, and no further instructions → execute diverse departure. Info in front of DOD approach plates.

## 24
**Q:** What is an ODP and when is it published?
**A:** Published instrument departure for obstacle avoidance when obstacles penetrate the 40:1 OIS; normally has min climb gradients and often non-standard takeoff weather minimums. Located in front of DOD approach plates.

## 25
**Q:** Do ODP non-standard weather minimums apply to USN/USMC?
**A:** No — they do not apply to USN/USMC aircraft. Those mins are normally for see-and-avoid; it is not advisable to use departures that require their use.

## 26
**Q:** Will ATC clear you for an ODP? What is the default?
**A:** ATC will not specifically clear you. Only one ODP per runway; it is the default in absence of radar vectors or SID. “Cleared as filed” with no further instructions → execute the ODP for the runway used.

## 27
**Q:** Where are SID graphic/written descriptions found?
**A:** With the Instrument Approach Plates at the rear of the airfield approaches.

## 28
**Q:** SID primary and secondary purposes; CNAF mandate?
**A:** Primary: obstacle clearance protection in IMC. Secondary: efficiency; reduce communications and departure delays. Not mandatory by CNAF M-3710.7 but use is encouraged.

## 29
**Q:** Give an example of a SID with higher takeoff weather than the runway’s PAR/ILS.
**A:** Some SIDs require higher takeoff wx (e.g., 300-1) even though the runway has a PAR/ILS needing 200-1/2.

## 30
**Q:** Pilot NAV SID — who navigates and how do you file? Example?
**A:** Pilot primarily navigates; minimum communication; enter SID identifier in DD-1801 route. Example: Kingsville Three with Corpus Christi transition → NQI3 CRP.

## 31
**Q:** Vector SID — who navigates and how do you file? Example?
**A:** ATC provides navigational guidance (hybrid SID/radar); enter identifier in route. Example: Chattanooga Seven Departure (VECTOR) → CHA7 CHA.

## 32
**Q:** When is a SID considered cancelled?
**A:** If established on a SID and subsequently vectored or cleared off the SID transition, SID is cancelled unless the controller adds “expect to resume SID.”

## 33
**Q:** How is a cancelled SID reinstated?
**A:** ATC must indicate the portion of the SID routing that still applies and restate altitude restrictions.

## 34
**Q:** Restated altitude on a SID — may you climb immediately?
**A:** Yes — climb immediately to the restated altitude unless ATC states altitude restrictions remain in effect; still follow the SID path-over-the-ground.

## 35
**Q:** Example DD-1801 SID route string from the lesson?
**A:** CHA7 CHA DCT RMG DCT MGM J39 CEW DCT INBRD DCT JAYDI DCT NPA.

## 36
**Q:** When is a radar departure used?
**A:** When there is no published SID, or you desire to proceed directly to the first point on the route of flight.

## 37
**Q:** How do you file a radar departure on the DD-1801?
**A:** Enter DCT + NAVAID/intersection as the first route entry; Remarks: “REQUEST RADAR DEPARTURE.”

## 38
**Q:** Besides radar departure, what else falls under Specific ATC Departure Instructions?
**A:** Verbal instructions — navigational guidance not based on radar.

## 39
**Q:** What is a VCOA?
**A:** Visual Climb Over Airport — climb in visual conditions to cross the airport or an on-airport NAVAID in a specified direction at/above a specified altitude when climb gradients/terrain preclude a conventional DP. Located in front of DOD approach plates.

## 40
**Q:** How do you file a VCOA?
**A:** VCOAs have no names or identifiers. File your first route point; in REMARKS state that you intend to depart via the VCOA.

## 41
**Q:** VFR Climb on Course — what do you do until IFR pickup?
**A:** Remain below Class A; fly VFR cruising altitudes and cloud clearances until issued an IFR clearance. ATC tells when/where to contact ARTCC.

## 42
**Q:** How do you file VFR Climb on Course? Example RMK?
**A:** Remarks: “REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE” (to where you enter the IFR enroute system). Example: RMK/REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE.

## 43
**Q:** Example VFR Climb on Course radio call from the lesson?
**A:** “Jacksonville Center, ROKT 21, VFR over Seminole at one zero thousand five hundred, looking to pickup my IFR flight plan to Navy Pensacola.”

## 44
**Q:** What is a STAR?
**A:** Pre-planned IFR arrival providing transition from the enroute structure to an outer fix or IAF.

## 45
**Q:** STAR altitude rule before “descend via”?
**A:** Maintain last assigned altitude until receiving authorization to descend so as to comply with all published/issued restrictions.

## 46
**Q:** What does a “DESCEND VIA” clearance authorize?
**A:** Lateral and vertical navigation in accordance with the depicted procedure to meet published restrictions.

## 47
**Q:** How do you refuse a STAR?
**A:** Indicate in DD-1801 Remarks (preferred), or on initial contact request “No STAR.”

## 48
**Q:** LEMIG ONE ARRIVAL — Cotulla Transition routing?
**A:** From over COT VORTAC via COT R-037 to LEMIG INT; thence… ALL AIRPORTS: from over LEMIG INT via SAT R-175 to ELKAY INT; expect radar vectors to final approach course.

## 49
**Q:** LEMIG ONE — turbojet vertical nav planning note at LEMIG?
**A:** Expect to cross LEMIG at 10,000′.

## 50
**Q:** LEMIG ONE — San Antonio Approach Control frequencies on the chart?
**A:** 125.7 and 381.4.

## 51
**Q:** What is UHF SFA?
**A:** Single Frequency Approach — single UHF frequency throughout approach to touchdown for military single-piloted turbojet aircraft at night or IMC; depicted in IFR Supplement; request from ATC.

## 52
**Q:** CNAF M-3710.7 SFA requirements?
**A:** Single-piloted aircraft on IFR SHALL be provided SFA to the maximum extent communication capabilities and traffic permit. Without SFA, keep frequency and/or transponder code shifts to an absolute minimum below 2500 ft AGL.

## 53
**Q:** Absolute single-piloted fixed-wing approach weather minimums?
**A:** 200 foot ceiling/HAT and 1/2 statute mile visibility / 2400 feet RVR.

## 54
**Q:** T-45 approach minimums rule?
**A:** 200-1/2 or as published, whichever is higher.

## 55
**Q:** Single-piloted straight-in absolute HAT and DA adjustment rule?
**A:** Absolute HAT 200 ft AGL. Adjust DH/DA so lowest point of descent is 200 HAT until you visually acquire the field — add (200 − published HAT) to the approach plate DA.

## 56
**Q:** KNQI S-PAR / HI-ILS example: published DA/HAT and single-piloted adjusted DA?
**A:** Published DA=150, HAT=100. Difference 100 (200−100) added to DA → single-piloted DA=250.

## 57
**Q:** May you commence an approach when dest/alternate weather is below minimums?
**A:** No — you may not commence an actual or practice instrument approach at destination/alternate when weather is reported below approach minimums.

## 58
**Q:** If weather goes below mins after you commence the approach?
**A:** Pilot may, at their discretion, continue the approach to published minimums.

## 59
**Q:** Practice approaches to minimums — where allowed?
**A:** At enroute airfields regardless of weather minimums. Not at filed destination or alternate — must have “no intent” to land.

## 60
**Q:** Formation IMC approach authorization for >2 aircraft?
**A:** Instrument approaches with or without intent to land in IMC by formations of more than two aircraft are not authorized. Penetration of IMC to obtain VMC by >2 aircraft is authorized.

## 61
**Q:** Two-aircraft formation approach weather minimums?
**A:** At least published circling minimums, or 1000-3 if circling approach is not authorized.

## 62
**Q:** Visual approach weather and sighting requirements?
**A:** 1000 & 3 at airport of intended landing; must have airfield OR preceding traffic in sight. If airfield but not preceding traffic, radar separation is provided.

## 63
**Q:** Who may request a visual approach?
**A:** Controller or pilot may request at any time.

## 64
**Q:** Is a visual approach an IAP? MAP? Flight plan effect?
**A:** Not an instrument approach, but an IFR procedure in VMC. No MAP. Acceptance does not cancel the flight plan.

## 65
**Q:** Visual approach cloud clearance note and ATC expectation?
**A:** Cloud clearances not applicable unless required by ops specs (slide refs 500 below / 1000 above / 2000 horizontal). Proceed visually most direct/safe to a normal straight-in final; contact Tower ASAP for go-around/MAP guidance.

## 66
**Q:** Who may request a contact approach, and what visibility is required?
**A:** Only the pilot may request. Reported 1 mile visibility; remain outside clouds; reasonable expectation of continuing to the airfield.

## 67
**Q:** Is a contact approach an IAP? MAP? Sample clearance?
**A:** Yes — it is an IAP and MAP will be provided. Example: “CLEARED CONTACT APPROACH, (at or below XXXX feet), ROUTE (if necessary), IF NOT POSSIBLE (MAP) AND ADVISE.”

## 68
**Q:** Contact approach altitude clearance rule?
**A:** No fixed altitude required, but if necessary cleared “at or below” an altitude at least 1000 ft below any IFR traffic and not below MSA. Radar separation provided.

## 69
**Q:** Circling approach: MDA and maneuvering rules?
**A:** Use circling mins. When runway environment in sight, state intentions and circle by any safe method unless a procedure/restrictions are specified. Do not descend below MDA to remain clear of clouds.

## 70
**Q:** If visual reference is lost while circling — actions?
**A:** Execute MAP for the original approach: immediate climb; initial turn toward the landing runway to stay in obstruction clearance area; continue turn until established on the missed approach course.

## 71
**Q:** When may you leave IAF altitude after radar vectors / DIRECT to IAF?
**A:** Maintain last assigned altitude until cleared for the approach and established on a segment of the published instrument approach; then you may descend. Diagram: Cross IAF → turn shortest direction → FAF.

## 72
**Q:** When is a missed approach conducted, and what must you tell ATC?
**A:** When at MDA and no visual reference exists at the MAP. Mandatory: inform ATC “executing missed approach.”

## 73
**Q:** NORDO missed approach to alternate — steps?
**A:** Squawk 7600; follow published missed approach; proceed to alternate as filed and immediately commence an approach. Consider filing a DRAFT if weather looks bad.

## 74
**Q:** What does filing a DRAFT give you?
**A:** A ROUTE clearance but not an ALTITUDE clearance (you have not received an ATC altitude clearance).

## 75
**Q:** NORDO altitude-to-alternate options if no expected altitude after DRAFT?
**A:** MSA out to 25 nm from each field; or Emergency Safe Altitudes if fields within 200 nm; or 18,000 ft MSL if altimeter ≥29.92, or FL 190 if altimeter <29.92 (any distance).

## 76
**Q:** What is a VDP?
**A:** Advisory defined point on the FAC from which normal descent from MDA to the touchdown area may be commenced with required visual reference. Used on selected nonprecision approaches where MAP would preclude a normal 3° (~300 ft/NM) path.

## 77
**Q:** Dive-and-drive vs constant-angle approach?
**A:** Dive-and-drive: descend to MDA after FAF then level to MAP. Constant-angle: continuous descent through the VDP toward MAP/touchdown.

## 78
**Q:** What is a VDA?
**A:** Visual Descent Angle — descent angle from FAF to the runway threshold at TCH.

## 79
**Q:** What is the VOR MON?
**A:** Minimum Operational Network of VORs providing conventional navigation backup if GPS/GNSS is lost. CONUS coverage at/above 5,000 ft AGL; supports oceanic arrivals and critical military use.

## 80
**Q:** MON airport approach capability and distance note?
**A:** Proceed to a MON airport and fly an ILS or VOR approach without GPS, DME, ADF, or surveillance. A MON airport will rarely be 100 NM distant.

## 81
**Q:** FLIP IFR Enroute Low Altitude — what do BLUE/GREEN vs BROWN airports mean?
**A:** BLUE or GREEN: approved IAP and/or RADAR MINIMA published (FAA TPP or DoD FLIP). BROWN: no published IAP or RADAR MINIMA. Military MON airports have a dedicated symbol row on the legend.

## 82
**Q:** How do you close a flight plan — military vs non-military?
**A:** Navy: verbal close with Tower or Base Ops at military facilities. Non-military: close with flight service by any means available.

## 83
**Q:** Log book “Cover Your Six” — what yearly minimums to track?
**A:** Medical; approaches; instrument/total flight time; types of flights. Maintain progress awareness; check accurate entries and that accomplished flights get logged.

## Airspeed Restrictions — General (FAR 91)

- **250 KIAS** maximum below **10,000 feet MSL** (FAR 91)
- To reduce midair collision hazards at low altitudes
- Level off above 10,000 feet MSL and slow up before going lower

**Exemptions (for DOD aircraft)**
- Within restricted areas
- Within MOAs
- Large scale exercises or on short term special mission
- Military developed **IFR/VFR** training routes
- Recommended safe maneuvering airspeed is greater
- National Defense Mission

---

## Airspeed Restrictions — Airspace classes

- **CLASS B:** 250 KIAS maximum
- **CLASS C:** 200 KIAS within **4 nm** of primary airport, from surface to **2500 ft AGL**
  - On-slide footprint note (under Class C bullet): **10 nm / 1200 ft AGL**; **20 nm / 4000 ft AGL** *[as printed on airspeed slide; may depict shelf-style dimensions]*
- **CLASS D:** 200 KIAS within **4 nm** of primary airport, from surface to **2500 ft AGL**
  - Individual footprint; 2500 ft AGL
- **200 KIAS** maximum under floors of Class B airspace; in a VFR corridor transiting Class B airspace

## Airspeed Restrictions — Holding

**Holding airspeeds**
- Hold at **MAXIMUM ENDURANCE** airspeed
- SOP for the **T-45 is 200 KIAS**

**Maximum Holding Airspeeds**
- **230 KIAS** at Navy airfields at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted
- **310 KIAS** at USAF airfields at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted
- **Civilian**
  - 200 KIAS … 0–6000 feet MSL
  - 230 KIAS … above 6000 feet MSL to/including 14000 feet MSL
  - 265 KIAS … above 14000 feet MSL

## IFR Departure Types — Overview (5+1)

**Types (5+1)**
1. Diverse Departure
2. Obstacle Departure Procedure (ODP)
3. Standard Instrument Departure (SID/DP) — two kinds: **Pilot NAV** and **Vector**
4. Specific ATC Departure Instructions
   - Radar Departure
   - Verbal instructions (navigational guidance **not** based on radar)
5. Visual Climb Over Airport (VCOA)
6. (+1) VFR Climb on Course

---

## Diverse Departure

- Airfield has at least one instrument approach procedure.
- No obstacles penetrate the **40:1** slope (obstacle identification surface / **OIS**).
- Fly runway heading until **400 ft** above the field elevation before executing any turns.
- Maintain a minimum climb gradient of **200 ft/nm** (unless a higher one is published) until reaching a minimum IFR altitude (MEA, MSA, etc.).

**“Will ATC clear me for a diverse departure?”**
- ATC will **not** specifically clear you.
- If you are **“Cleared as filed”**, there is no minimum published climb gradient or **“Terrible T”** for the departure runway, and ATC does not issue further instructions, then ATC expects you to execute a diverse departure.
- Located in front of DOD approach plates

---

## IFR (Obstacle) Departure Procedure (ODP)

- Published instrument departure procedures for obstacle avoidance (obstacles penetrate 40:1 slope of Obstacle Identification Surface [OIS])
- Normally will have minimum climb gradients and non-standard takeoff weather minimums as well
- **Non-standard weather minimums do not apply to USN/USMC aircraft.**
- Non-standard weather minimums normally provided to “see-and-avoid” obstacles. It is not advisable to use such departures that require their use.
- Located in the front of DOD approach plates

**“Will ATC clear me for an ODP?”**
- ATC will **not** specifically clear you.
- Only **one** ODP is established for a runway.
- It is considered to be the **default** departure procedure and is intended for use in absence of radar vectors or SID.
- If you are **“Cleared as filed”**, and ATC does not issue further instructions, then ATC expects you to execute the ODP for the runway used.

---

## Standard Instrument Departure (SID)

- Pre-planned IFR Air Traffic Control procedure
- Graphic and written descriptions are found with the Instrument Approach Plates at **rear** of the airfield approaches.
- Usually no altitudes depicted. Altitudes will be issued with clearance.
- Sometimes there are altitude instructions but normally say to cross a specific location at/above/below a specific altitude in conjunction with clearance altitude.
- Some have minimum climb rates.
- Some have higher takeoff weather minimums than otherwise would normally prevail (e.g., **300-1** even though the runway has a PAR/ILS needing **200-1/2**).
- **Primary purpose:** provide obstacle clearance protection for aircraft in IMC.
- **Secondary purpose:** increase efficiency and reduce communications and departure delays.
- **Not mandatory** by CNAF M-3710.7 but use is encouraged.

### Pilot NAV SID
- Pilot is primarily responsible for navigation.
- Minimum communication
- On DD-1801 flight plan enter identifier in the route section.
- Example: Kingsville Three Departure (NAS Kingsville) with a Corpus Christi transition (SW Plates) → **NQI3 CRP**

### Vector SID
- ATC provides navigational guidance.
- Hybrid SID/RADAR Vector
- On DD-1801 flight plan enter identifier in the route section.
- Example: Chattanooga Seven Departure (VECTOR) → **CHA7 CHA**

### SID cancelled / altitude changes
- After an aircraft is established on a SID and subsequently vectored or cleared off of the SID transition, pilots shall consider the **SID cancelled**, unless the controller adds **“expect to resume SID.”**
- For a cancelled SID to be reinstated, ATC must indicate the portion of the SID routing that still applies and **restate altitude restrictions**.
- If an altitude to maintain is changed or restated, either prior to or after departure, you can **climb immediately** to that altitude unless ATC states that **altitude restrictions remain in effect** (in this case, they would only be changing your ultimate altitude).
- The SID **path-over-the-ground** must still be followed even though a climb to higher altitude has commenced.

### SID Entry (Route of Flight Block) — DD-1801 example (recovered)
- Example route string: **`CHA7 CHA DCT RMG DCT MGM J39 CEW DCT INBRD DCT JAYDI DCT NPA`**
- Form fields visible on slide (partial): Message Type / Aircraft ID / Flight Rules; Number/Type/Wake; Equipment; Departure aerodrome **KCHA** time; Cruising speed **N0333** level **F240**; Destination **KNPA**; alts **KMOB** / etc.; Other Information includes **DOF/… REG/USN OPR/DOD PER/C**

**Used when:**
- No published SID
- Desire to proceed directly to the first point on route of flight

**Filing:**
- On DD-1801 enter **DCT** followed by the NAVAID or intersection that will establish you on your route as the first entry.

**Also under Specific ATC:** Verbal instructions (navigational guidance not based on radar) — listed separately from radar departure in the types overview.

## Visual Climb Over Airport (VCOA)

- Aircraft is unable to meet required climb gradients for departure procedures.
- Airports at which a conventional instrument departure procedure is impossible to design due to terrain or other obstacle hazard
- Requires a climb in **visual conditions** to cross the airport or an on-airport NAVAID in a specified direction, at or above a specified altitude
- Located in front of DOD approach plates

**How to File a VCOA**
- VCOAs have **no names or identifiers**.
- File your first point on the route of flight.
- In **REMARKS** section state that you intend to depart via the VCOA.

## VFR Climb On Course (+1)

- Used when wish to remain VFR until reaching a point further along route of travel
- ATC will issue instructions as to when/where to contact ARTCC for your IFR clearance.
- Example radio call: *“Jacksonville Center, ROKT 21, VFR over Seminole at one zero thousand five hundred, looking to pickup my IFR flight plan to Navy Pensacola.”*
- Remain below Class A airspace, fly VFR cruising altitudes/cloud clearances until issued IFR clearance.
- Enter **"REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE"** (to location where entering the IFR enroute system) in the REMARKS section.
- DD-1801 Remarks example recovered: **`RMK/REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE`**

## Standard Terminal Arrival (STAR)

- Pre-planned instrument flight rule arrival
- Provides transition from the enroute structure to an outer fix **or** IAF
- Pilots navigating on a STAR shall maintain last assigned altitude until receiving authorization to descend so as to comply with all published/issued restrictions.
- Authorization to follow descent instructions will contain the phraseology **“DESCEND VIA.”**
- A **“descend via”** clearance authorizes a pilot to vertically and laterally navigate, in accordance with the depicted procedure, to meet published restrictions.

**If you do not want a STAR:**
- Indicate in DD-1801 Remarks section (preferred method)
- On initial contact request **“No STAR”**

## Instrument Approaches — UHF Single Frequency Approach (SFA)

- Depicted in IFR Supplement.
- Request from ATC

## Instrument Approaches — Single-Piloted Weather / HAT Minimums

- Absolute weather minimums for a single-piloted fixed-wing aircraft executing an approach are **200 foot ceiling/HAT** and **1/2 statute mile visibility / 2400 feet RVR**.
- **T-45 approach mins are 200-1/2 or as published, whichever is higher.**

**Single-Piloted Straight-In Approach HAT Minimums**
- Absolute Height Above Touchdown (HAT) minimum is **200 feet AGL**.
- DH/DA must be adjusted, if necessary, so as to provide 200 feet height above touchdown as your lowest point of descent unless or until you visually acquire the field.

## Instrument Approaches — Commencing / practice / formation

- You may **not commence** an actual or practice instrument approach at your destination/alternate when the weather is reported **below approach minimums**.
- If the airfield goes below minimums **after** an approach has been commenced, pilot may, at their discretion, **continue** the approach to published minimums.
- Practice approaches to minimums may be conducted at **enroute** airfields regardless of weather minimums. However, you cannot make a “practice” approach at your filed destination or alternate. Must have **“no intent”** to land.

**Formation**
- Instrument approaches with or without intent to land in IMC by formations of **more than two** aircraft are **not authorized**.
- Penetration of IMC to obtain VMC by formations of more than two aircraft **is** authorized.
- Approach weather minimums for **two** aircraft must be at least published circling minimums or **1000 - 3** if circling approach is not authorized.

---

## Instrument Approach Modifications — Visual Approach

- An ATC authorization for an aircraft on an IFR flight plan to proceed visually and clear of clouds to the airport of intended landing
- Controller or pilot may request at anytime
- **1000 & 3** required at airport of intended landing
- Must have airfield **OR** preceding traffic in sight
- If airfield, but not preceding traffic in sight, radar separation is provided.
- When visually following a preceding aircraft, pilot is responsible for maintaining a safe approach interval and adequate wake turbulence separation.
- **Not** an instrument approach but it is an IFR procedure conducted under Instrument Flight Rules in VMC.
- **No MAP**
- Remain clear of clouds; contact ATC (Tower) ASAP for “go-around” or Missed Approach guidance.
- Cloud clearances are not applicable unless required by operational specifications. (Reference values on slide: **500 ft below / 1000 ft above / 2000 ft horizontal**)
- Does **not** cancel a flight plan by its acceptance.
- ATC expects you to proceed visually in the most direct and safe manner to a normal straight-in final approach.

---

## Instrument Approach Modifications — Contact Approach

- **Only the pilot** may request
- An ATC authorization for an aircraft on an IFR flight plan
- May proceed by visual reference to the surface if reported **1 mile visibility**, be able to remain outside of clouds, and have a reasonable expectation of continuing to the airfield in these conditions
- It **is** an instrument approach procedure — **MAP will be provided**.
- Phraseology example: “CLEARED CONTACT APPROACH, (at or below XXXX feet), ROUTE (if necessary), IF NOT POSSIBLE (MAP) AND ADVISE.”
- Altitude clearance: no fixed altitude, but if necessary, will be cleared “at or below” an altitude which is at least **1000 feet below any IFR traffic** but **not below MSA**
- Radar separation provided

---

## Circling Approach

- Use circling minimums.
- When runway environment is in sight, state intentions to approach control and visually circle to land on the intended runway by any safe method unless a circling procedure is specified or there are restrictions.
- Do **not** descend below MDA to remain clear of clouds.

**If visual reference is lost**
- Execute MAP for original approach.
- Initiate an immediate climb.
- An initial turn toward the landing runway to ensure remaining within obstruction clearance area
- Continue to turn until established on the missed approach course.

**Diagram notes (frames 114 / 116)**
- Title graphic: “CIRCLING APPROACHES”
- Lost-visual diagram: “Visual Reference Lost Here” with turn toward landing runway then join **Published Missed Approach** course (from either side of runway)

## Leaving IAF Altitude

- If being RADAR vectored or cleared **“DIRECT”** to an IAF, maintain last assigned altitude.
- When subsequently cleared for the approach, you may descend once **established on a segment** of the published instrument approach.

**Diagram (frame 119):** Cross IAF → Turn Shortest Direction → FAF. Caption: **RADAR VECTORED OR CLEARED DIRECT**

## Missed Approach

- Conducted when established at Minimum Descent Altitude and a visual reference does not exist when reaching missed approach point (MAP)
- It is mandatory to inform ATC: **“executing missed approach.”**

## NORDO Missed Approach to Alternate

- Squawk **7600**
- Follow published missed approach procedures
- Proceed to alternate as filed and immediately commence an approach.
- Consider filing a **DRAFT** with ATC if weather is looking bad; constitutes a **ROUTE** clearance but **not** an **ALTITUDE** clearance since you have not received an ATC clearance.

**Altitude to Alternate**
- Fly the expected altitude if given one after filing a DRAFT; otherwise (your option):
  - **MINIMUM SAFE ALTITUDE** out to 25 nm from each field
  - **EMERGENCY SAFE ALTITUDES** if fields are within 200 nm of each other, or
  - At **18,000 feet MSL** if altimeter is ≥ 29.92, or **FL 190** if altimeter is < 29.92 (any distance)

## Visual Descent Point (VDP) / VDA

**VDP**
- Defined point on the FAC from which normal descent from the MDA to runway touchdown area may be commenced (with required visual reference)
- Incorporated into selected nonprecision approach procedures where the MAP would put you in a position where you cannot make a normal 3-degree descent to the touchdown area.
- Normally will provide a **3 degree** glideslope (**300 ft per NM**)
- Strictly **advisory**

**Profile example (frame 126 — partial MAP text recovered)**
- Missed approach snippet: To **2500** out **R-332** to **12 DME** then to **3000** to **16 DME** and hold

**“Dive and Drive” vs “Constant Angle” Approach (frames 127–128)**
- Dive-and-drive: descend to MDA after FAF, level to MAP
- Constant-angle: continuous descent through **Visual Descent Point** toward MAP / touchdown
- Labels: FAF → Visual Descent Point → Missed Approach Point; MDH referenced

**VDP vs VDA (Visual Descent Angle)**
- **VDP:** Location where normal rate of descent (~**300 ft/NM**; slide also OCR’d “~300 fpm” on one frame — treat as **ft/NM** for 3°) to the runway touchdown point may be commenced
- **VDA:** Descent angle from FAF to the runway threshold at **TCH**
- Example plate note recovered: TCH / descent-angle table present; “descent angles … not coincident” style caution visible on sample chart

## Minimum Operational Network (MON)

**What is the VOR “MON”?**
- The Very high frequency Omni-directional Range (VOR) Minimum Operational Network (MON) provides a conventional navigation backup service in the event of a loss of Global Positioning System (GPS) signal.
- The MON includes the minimum number of geographically situated VORs in the Contiguous United States (CONUS) necessary to provide coverage at and above **5,000 feet** above ground level.
- Additionally, the MON supports international oceanic arrival routes and mission critical military use.

- The VOR MON is designed to enable aircraft, having lost Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) service, to revert to conventional navigation procedures.
- The VOR MON is further designed to allow aircraft to proceed to a MON airport where an **ILS or VOR** approach procedure can be flown without the necessity of GPS, DME, ADF, or surveillance.
- A MON airport will rarely be **100 NM** distant.

### FLIP IFR Enroute Low Altitude legend (frames 134–135) — MON symbology *[re-OCR]*
