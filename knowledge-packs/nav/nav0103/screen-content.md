## Airspeed Restrictions — General (FAR 91)

- **250 KIAS** maximum below **10,000 feet MSL** (FAR 91)
- To reduce midair collision hazards at low altitudes
- Level off above 10,000 feet MSL and slow up before going lower

**Exemptions (for DOD aircraft)**
- Within restricted areas
- Within MOAs
- Large scale exercises or on short term special mission
- Military developed **IFR/VFR** training routes
- Recommended safe maneuvering airspeed is greater
- National Defense Mission

---

## Airspeed Restrictions — Airspace classes

- **CLASS B:** 250 KIAS maximum
- **CLASS C:** 200 KIAS within **4 nm** of primary airport, from surface to **2500 ft AGL**
  - On-slide footprint note (under Class C bullet): **10 nm / 1200 ft AGL**; **20 nm / 4000 ft AGL** *[as printed on airspeed slide; may depict shelf-style dimensions]*
- **CLASS D:** 200 KIAS within **4 nm** of primary airport, from surface to **2500 ft AGL**
  - Individual footprint; 2500 ft AGL
- **200 KIAS** maximum under floors of Class B airspace; in a VFR corridor transiting Class B airspace

---

## Airspeed Restrictions — Holding

**Holding airspeeds**
- Hold at **MAXIMUM ENDURANCE** airspeed
- SOP for the **T-45 is 200 KIAS**

**Maximum Holding Airspeeds**
- **230 KIAS** at Navy airfields at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted
- **310 KIAS** at USAF airfields at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted
- **Civilian**
  - 200 KIAS … 0–6000 feet MSL
  - 230 KIAS … above 6000 feet MSL to/including 14000 feet MSL
  - 265 KIAS … above 14000 feet MSL

## Fuel Requirements

- **CNAF M-3710.7** requires a fuel reserve for all aircraft of **10% of planned requirements** or **20 minutes of flight** based on maximum endurance at **10,000 feet MSL**, whichever is greater.
- Fuel reserve for the **T-45 is ALWAYS 500 pounds IFR** (TW-1/TW-2 joint SOP).

## Weather Criteria for Takeoff — Special Instrument Card

- No ceiling or visibility minimums apply
- Takeoff shall depend on the judgment of the pilot and urgency of the flight.
- **Requirements**
  - 5 years flight experience
  - 2000 hours of flight time
  - 100 hours actual instrument time
- Minimums may be reduced

---

## Weather Criteria for Takeoff — Standard Instrument Card

- May base take-off criteria on **ANY APPROACH** compatible with installed and operable aircraft equipment (**TAC/VOR/ILS/LOC/PAR/ASR**)
- **Non-precision Approach:** Published minimums but not less than **300 - 1**
- **Precision Approach:** Published minimums but not less than **200 - 1/2**

---

## Weather Criteria for Takeoff — Formations

- Equal or above published circling minimums for runway in use
- If circling approach not authorized/exists, then need **VFR of 1000 - 3**

## IFR Departure Types — Overview (5+1)

**Types (5+1)**
1. Diverse Departure
2. Obstacle Departure Procedure (ODP)
3. Standard Instrument Departure (SID/DP) — two kinds: **Pilot NAV** and **Vector**
4. Specific ATC Departure Instructions
   - Radar Departure
   - Verbal instructions (navigational guidance **not** based on radar)
5. Visual Climb Over Airport (VCOA)
6. (+1) VFR Climb on Course

---

## Diverse Departure

- Airfield has at least one instrument approach procedure.
- No obstacles penetrate the **40:1** slope (obstacle identification surface / **OIS**).
- Fly runway heading until **400 ft** above the field elevation before executing any turns.
- Maintain a minimum climb gradient of **200 ft/nm** (unless a higher one is published) until reaching a minimum IFR altitude (MEA, MSA, etc.).

**“Will ATC clear me for a diverse departure?”**
- ATC will **not** specifically clear you.
- If you are **“Cleared as filed”**, there is no minimum published climb gradient or **“Terrible T”** for the departure runway, and ATC does not issue further instructions, then ATC expects you to execute a diverse departure.
- Located in front of DOD approach plates

---

## IFR (Obstacle) Departure Procedure (ODP)

- Published instrument departure procedures for obstacle avoidance (obstacles penetrate 40:1 slope of Obstacle Identification Surface [OIS])
- Normally will have minimum climb gradients and non-standard takeoff weather minimums as well
- **Non-standard weather minimums do not apply to USN/USMC aircraft.**
- Non-standard weather minimums normally provided to “see-and-avoid” obstacles. It is not advisable to use such departures that require their use.
- Located in the front of DOD approach plates

**“Will ATC clear me for an ODP?”**
- ATC will **not** specifically clear you.
- Only **one** ODP is established for a runway.
- It is considered to be the **default** departure procedure and is intended for use in absence of radar vectors or SID.
- If you are **“Cleared as filed”**, and ATC does not issue further instructions, then ATC expects you to execute the ODP for the runway used.

## Standard Instrument Departure (SID)

- Pre-planned IFR Air Traffic Control procedure
- Graphic and written descriptions are found with the Instrument Approach Plates at **rear** of the airfield approaches.
- Usually no altitudes depicted. Altitudes will be issued with clearance.
- Sometimes there are altitude instructions but normally say to cross a specific location at/above/below a specific altitude in conjunction with clearance altitude.
- Some have minimum climb rates.
- Some have higher takeoff weather minimums than otherwise would normally prevail (e.g., **300-1** even though the runway has a PAR/ILS needing **200-1/2**).
- **Primary purpose:** provide obstacle clearance protection for aircraft in IMC.
- **Secondary purpose:** increase efficiency and reduce communications and departure delays.
- **Not mandatory** by CNAF M-3710.7 but use is encouraged.

### Pilot NAV SID
- Pilot is primarily responsible for navigation.
- Minimum communication
- On DD-1801 flight plan enter identifier in the route section.
- Example: Kingsville Three Departure (NAS Kingsville) with a Corpus Christi transition (SW Plates) → **NQI3 CRP**

### Vector SID
- ATC provides navigational guidance.
- Hybrid SID/RADAR Vector
- On DD-1801 flight plan enter identifier in the route section.
- Example: Chattanooga Seven Departure (VECTOR) → **CHA7 CHA**

### SID cancelled / altitude changes
- After an aircraft is established on a SID and subsequently vectored or cleared off of the SID transition, pilots shall consider the **SID cancelled**, unless the controller adds **“expect to resume SID.”**
- For a cancelled SID to be reinstated, ATC must indicate the portion of the SID routing that still applies and **restate altitude restrictions**.
- If an altitude to maintain is changed or restated, either prior to or after departure, you can **climb immediately** to that altitude unless ATC states that **altitude restrictions remain in effect** (in this case, they would only be changing your ultimate altitude).
- The SID **path-over-the-ground** must still be followed even though a climb to higher altitude has commenced.

### SID Entry (Route of Flight Block) — DD-1801 example (recovered)
- Example route string: **`CHA7 CHA DCT RMG DCT MGM J39 CEW DCT INBRD DCT JAYDI DCT NPA`**
- Form fields visible on slide (partial): Message Type / Aircraft ID / Flight Rules; Number/Type/Wake; Equipment; Departure aerodrome **KCHA** time; Cruising speed **N0333** level **F240**; Destination **KNPA**; alts **KMOB** / etc.; Other Information includes **DOF/… REG/USN OPR/DOD PER/C**

## Specific ATC Departure Instructions — Radar Departure

**Used when:**
- No published SID
- Desire to proceed directly to the first point on route of flight

**Filing:**
- On DD-1801 enter **DCT** followed by the NAVAID or intersection that will establish you on your route as the first entry.
- Enter **"REQUEST RADAR DEPARTURE"** in the Remarks section.

**Also under Specific ATC:** Verbal instructions (navigational guidance not based on radar) — listed separately from radar departure in the types overview.

---

## Visual Climb Over Airport (VCOA)

- Aircraft is unable to meet required climb gradients for departure procedures.
- Airports at which a conventional instrument departure procedure is impossible to design due to terrain or other obstacle hazard
- Requires a climb in **visual conditions** to cross the airport or an on-airport NAVAID in a specified direction, at or above a specified altitude
- Located in front of DOD approach plates

**How to File a VCOA**
- VCOAs have **no names or identifiers**.
- File your first point on the route of flight.
- In **REMARKS** section state that you intend to depart via the VCOA.

---

## VFR Climb On Course (+1)

- Used when wish to remain VFR until reaching a point further along route of travel
- ATC will issue instructions as to when/where to contact ARTCC for your IFR clearance.
- Example radio call: *“Jacksonville Center, ROKT 21, VFR over Seminole at one zero thousand five hundred, looking to pickup my IFR flight plan to Navy Pensacola.”*
- Remain below Class A airspace, fly VFR cruising altitudes/cloud clearances until issued IFR clearance.
- Enter **"REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE"** (to location where entering the IFR enroute system) in the REMARKS section.
- DD-1801 Remarks example recovered: **`RMK/REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE`**

---

## Standard Terminal Arrival (STAR)

- Pre-planned instrument flight rule arrival
- Provides transition from the enroute structure to an outer fix **or** IAF
- Pilots navigating on a STAR shall maintain last assigned altitude until receiving authorization to descend so as to comply with all published/issued restrictions.
- Authorization to follow descent instructions will contain the phraseology **“DESCEND VIA.”**
- A **“descend via”** clearance authorizes a pilot to vertically and laterally navigate, in accordance with the depicted procedure, to meet published restrictions.

**If you do not want a STAR:**
- Indicate in DD-1801 Remarks section (preferred method)
- On initial contact request **“No STAR”**

## Instrument Approaches — UHF Single Frequency Approach (SFA)

- Depicted in IFR Supplement.
- Request from ATC
- **CNAF M-3710.7:** “Single piloted aircraft arriving on an IFR flight plan SHALL be provided SFA to the maximum extent that communication capabilities and traffic permit.”
- “Those activities without SFA capabilities SHALL keep frequency and/or transponder code shifts to an absolute minimum below **2500 ft AGL**.”
- Service provided to military single-piloted turbojet aircraft at night or IMC.
- Single UHF frequency throughout approach to touchdown

## Instrument Approaches — Single-Piloted Weather / HAT Minimums

- Absolute weather minimums for a single-piloted fixed-wing aircraft executing an approach are **200 foot ceiling/HAT** and **1/2 statute mile visibility / 2400 feet RVR**.
- **T-45 approach mins are 200-1/2 or as published, whichever is higher.**

**Single-Piloted Straight-In Approach HAT Minimums**
- Absolute Height Above Touchdown (HAT) minimum is **200 feet AGL**.
- DH/DA must be adjusted, if necessary, so as to provide 200 feet height above touchdown as your lowest point of descent unless or until you visually acquire the field.

## Instrument Approaches — Commencing / practice / formation

- You may **not commence** an actual or practice instrument approach at your destination/alternate when the weather is reported **below approach minimums**.
- If the airfield goes below minimums **after** an approach has been commenced, pilot may, at their discretion, **continue** the approach to published minimums.
- Practice approaches to minimums may be conducted at **enroute** airfields regardless of weather minimums. However, you cannot make a “practice” approach at your filed destination or alternate. Must have **“no intent”** to land.

**Formation**
- Instrument approaches with or without intent to land in IMC by formations of **more than two** aircraft are **not authorized**.
- Penetration of IMC to obtain VMC by formations of more than two aircraft **is** authorized.
- Approach weather minimums for **two** aircraft must be at least published circling minimums or **1000 - 3** if circling approach is not authorized.

## Instrument Approach Modifications — Visual Approach

- An ATC authorization for an aircraft on an IFR flight plan to proceed visually and clear of clouds to the airport of intended landing
- Controller or pilot may request at anytime
- **1000 & 3** required at airport of intended landing
- Must have airfield **OR** preceding traffic in sight
- If airfield, but not preceding traffic in sight, radar separation is provided.
- When visually following a preceding aircraft, pilot is responsible for maintaining a safe approach interval and adequate wake turbulence separation.
- **Not** an instrument approach but it is an IFR procedure conducted under Instrument Flight Rules in VMC.
- **No MAP**
- Remain clear of clouds; contact ATC (Tower) ASAP for “go-around” or Missed Approach guidance.
- Cloud clearances are not applicable unless required by operational specifications. (Reference values on slide: **500 ft below / 1000 ft above / 2000 ft horizontal**)
- Does **not** cancel a flight plan by its acceptance.
- ATC expects you to proceed visually in the most direct and safe manner to a normal straight-in final approach.

---

## Instrument Approach Modifications — Contact Approach

- **Only the pilot** may request
- An ATC authorization for an aircraft on an IFR flight plan
- May proceed by visual reference to the surface if reported **1 mile visibility**, be able to remain outside of clouds, and have a reasonable expectation of continuing to the airfield in these conditions
- It **is** an instrument approach procedure — **MAP will be provided**.
- Phraseology example: “CLEARED CONTACT APPROACH, (at or below XXXX feet), ROUTE (if necessary), IF NOT POSSIBLE (MAP) AND ADVISE.”
- Altitude clearance: no fixed altitude, but if necessary, will be cleared “at or below” an altitude which is at least **1000 feet below any IFR traffic** but **not below MSA**
- Radar separation provided

---

## Circling Approach

- Use circling minimums.
- When runway environment is in sight, state intentions to approach control and visually circle to land on the intended runway by any safe method unless a circling procedure is specified or there are restrictions.
- Do **not** descend below MDA to remain clear of clouds.

**If visual reference is lost**
- Execute MAP for original approach.
- Initiate an immediate climb.
- An initial turn toward the landing runway to ensure remaining within obstruction clearance area
- Continue to turn until established on the missed approach course.

**Diagram notes (frames 114 / 116)**
- Title graphic: “CIRCLING APPROACHES”
- Lost-visual diagram: “Visual Reference Lost Here” with turn toward landing runway then join **Published Missed Approach** course (from either side of runway)

## Leaving IAF Altitude

- If being RADAR vectored or cleared **“DIRECT”** to an IAF, maintain last assigned altitude.
- When subsequently cleared for the approach, you may descend once **established on a segment** of the published instrument approach.

**Diagram (frame 119):** Cross IAF → Turn Shortest Direction → FAF. Caption: **RADAR VECTORED OR CLEARED DIRECT**

## Missed Approach

- Conducted when established at Minimum Descent Altitude and a visual reference does not exist when reaching missed approach point (MAP)
- It is mandatory to inform ATC: **“executing missed approach.”**

---

## NORDO Missed Approach to Alternate

- Squawk **7600**
- Follow published missed approach procedures
- Proceed to alternate as filed and immediately commence an approach.
- Consider filing a **DRAFT** with ATC if weather is looking bad; constitutes a **ROUTE** clearance but **not** an **ALTITUDE** clearance since you have not received an ATC clearance.

**Altitude to Alternate**
- Fly the expected altitude if given one after filing a DRAFT; otherwise (your option):
  - **MINIMUM SAFE ALTITUDE** out to 25 nm from each field
  - **EMERGENCY SAFE ALTITUDES** if fields are within 200 nm of each other, or
  - At **18,000 feet MSL** if altimeter is ≥ 29.92, or **FL 190** if altimeter is < 29.92 (any distance)

## Visual Descent Point (VDP) / VDA

**VDP**
- Defined point on the FAC from which normal descent from the MDA to runway touchdown area may be commenced (with required visual reference)
- Incorporated into selected nonprecision approach procedures where the MAP would put you in a position where you cannot make a normal 3-degree descent to the touchdown area.
- Normally will provide a **3 degree** glideslope (**300 ft per NM**)
- Strictly **advisory**

**Profile example (frame 126 — partial MAP text recovered)**
- Missed approach snippet: To **2500** out **R-332** to **12 DME** then to **3000** to **16 DME** and hold

**“Dive and Drive” vs “Constant Angle” Approach (frames 127–128)**
- Dive-and-drive: descend to MDA after FAF, level to MAP
- Constant-angle: continuous descent through **Visual Descent Point** toward MAP / touchdown
- Labels: FAF → Visual Descent Point → Missed Approach Point; MDH referenced

**VDP vs VDA (Visual Descent Angle)**
- **VDP:** Location where normal rate of descent (~**300 ft/NM**; slide also OCR’d “~300 fpm” on one frame — treat as **ft/NM** for 3°) to the runway touchdown point may be commenced
- **VDA:** Descent angle from FAF to the runway threshold at **TCH**
- Example plate note recovered: TCH / descent-angle table present; “descent angles … not coincident” style caution visible on sample chart

## Minimum Operational Network (MON)

**What is the VOR “MON”?**
- The Very high frequency Omni-directional Range (VOR) Minimum Operational Network (MON) provides a conventional navigation backup service in the event of a loss of Global Positioning System (GPS) signal.
- The MON includes the minimum number of geographically situated VORs in the Contiguous United States (CONUS) necessary to provide coverage at and above **5,000 feet** above ground level.
- Additionally, the MON supports international oceanic arrival routes and mission critical military use.

- The VOR MON is designed to enable aircraft, having lost Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) service, to revert to conventional navigation procedures.
- The VOR MON is further designed to allow aircraft to proceed to a MON airport where an **ILS or VOR** approach procedure can be flown without the necessity of GPS, DME, ADF, or surveillance.
- A MON airport will rarely be **100 NM** distant.

### FLIP IFR Enroute Low Altitude legend (frames 134–135) — MON symbology *[re-OCR]*
- Publication: United States Government **Flight Information Publication** — **IFR ENROUTE LOW ALTITUDE - U.S.**
- For use up to but not including **18,000′ MSL**; Horizontal Datum: NAD 83
- Airport color coding: BLUE or GREEN = approved IAP and/or RADAR MINIMA published (FAA TPP or DoD FLIP); BLUE at least in High Altitude DoD FLIP; BROWN = no published IAP or RADAR MINIMA
