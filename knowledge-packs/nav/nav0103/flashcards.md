# Flashcards — NAV0103 Departure and Terminal Procedures

## 1
**Q:** What is the FAR 91 maximum airspeed below 10,000 ft MSL, and why?
**A:** 250 KIAS maximum — to reduce midair collision hazards at low altitudes. Level off above 10,000 and slow before descending lower.

## 2
**Q:** Name the six DOD exemptions to the 250 KIAS below 10,000 rule.
**A:** Within restricted areas; within MOAs; large-scale exercises or short-term special mission; military IFR/VFR training routes; when recommended safe maneuvering airspeed is greater; National Defense Mission.

## 3
**Q:** Class B airspeed limit?
**A:** 250 KIAS maximum.

## 4
**Q:** Class C airspeed limit near the primary airport?
**A:** 200 KIAS within 4 nm of the primary airport, from the surface to 2500 ft AGL.

## 5
**Q:** What Class C footprint dimensions appear as a note on the airspeed slide?
**A:** 10 nm / 1200 ft AGL and 20 nm / 4000 ft AGL (as printed under the Class C bullet).

## 6
**Q:** Class D airspeed limit near the primary airport?
**A:** 200 KIAS within 4 nm of the primary airport, from the surface to 2500 ft AGL; individual footprint, typically to 2500 ft AGL.

## 7
**Q:** Where else is 200 KIAS the maximum besides Class C/D core?
**A:** Under floors of Class B airspace, and in a VFR corridor transiting Class B airspace.

## 8
**Q:** At what airspeed should you hold, and what is T-45 SOP?
**A:** Hold at MAXIMUM ENDURANCE airspeed; T-45 SOP is 200 KIAS.

## 9
**Q:** Maximum holding airspeed at Navy airfields?
**A:** 230 KIAS at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted.

## 10
**Q:** Maximum holding airspeed at USAF airfields?
**A:** 310 KIAS at all altitudes unless otherwise depicted.

## 11
**Q:** Civilian maximum holding airspeeds by altitude band?
**A:** 200 KIAS from 0–6000 ft MSL; 230 KIAS above 6000 to/including 14,000 ft MSL; 265 KIAS above 14,000 ft MSL.

## 12
**Q:** CNAF M-3710.7 fuel reserve requirement?
**A:** Greater of 10% of planned requirements or 20 minutes of flight at maximum endurance at 10,000 ft MSL.

## 13
**Q:** T-45 IFR fuel reserve per TW-1/TW-2 joint SOP?
**A:** ALWAYS 500 pounds IFR.

## 14
**Q:** Special Instrument Card takeoff weather rule?
**A:** No ceiling or visibility minimums apply; takeoff depends on pilot judgment and urgency of the flight. Minimums may be reduced.

## 15
**Q:** Special Instrument Card qualification requirements?
**A:** 5 years flight experience; 2000 hours flight time; 100 hours actual instrument time.

## 16
**Q:** What approaches may Standard Instrument Card takeoff weather be based on?
**A:** ANY approach compatible with installed and operable equipment: TAC/VOR/ILS/LOC/PAR/ASR.

## 17
**Q:** Standard Instrument Card non-precision takeoff weather floor?
**A:** Published minimums but not less than 300-1.

## 18
**Q:** Standard Instrument Card precision takeoff weather floor?
**A:** Published minimums but not less than 200-1/2.

## 19
**Q:** Formation takeoff weather criteria?
**A:** Equal or above published circling minimums for the runway in use; if circling not authorized/exists, need VFR 1000-3.

## 20
**Q:** List the IFR departure types (5+1).
**A:** Diverse; Obstacle DP (ODP); SID/DP (Pilot NAV and Vector); Specific ATC (Radar Departure and verbal non-radar instructions); VCOA; (+1) VFR Climb on Course.

## 21
**Q:** Diverse departure prerequisites regarding IAP and OIS?
**A:** Airfield has at least one IAP, and no obstacles penetrate the 40:1 Obstacle Identification Surface (OIS).

## 22
**Q:** Diverse departure heading and climb gradient rules?
**A:** Fly runway heading until 400 ft above field elevation before any turns; maintain minimum climb 200 ft/nm (unless higher published) to a minimum IFR altitude (MEA, MSA, etc.).

## 23
**Q:** When does ATC expect a diverse departure?
**A:** ATC will not specifically clear you. If “Cleared as filed,” no published min climb gradient or “Terrible T” for the departure runway, and no further instructions → execute diverse departure. Info in front of DOD approach plates.

## 24
**Q:** What is an ODP and when is it published?
**A:** Published instrument departure for obstacle avoidance when obstacles penetrate the 40:1 OIS; normally has min climb gradients and often non-standard takeoff weather minimums. Located in front of DOD approach plates.

## 25
**Q:** Do ODP non-standard weather minimums apply to USN/USMC?
**A:** No — they do not apply to USN/USMC aircraft. Those mins are normally for see-and-avoid; it is not advisable to use departures that require their use.

## 26
**Q:** Will ATC clear you for an ODP? What is the default?
**A:** ATC will not specifically clear you. Only one ODP per runway; it is the default in absence of radar vectors or SID. “Cleared as filed” with no further instructions → execute the ODP for the runway used.

## 27
**Q:** Where are SID graphic/written descriptions found?
**A:** With the Instrument Approach Plates at the rear of the airfield approaches.

## 28
**Q:** SID primary and secondary purposes; CNAF mandate?
**A:** Primary: obstacle clearance protection in IMC. Secondary: efficiency; reduce communications and departure delays. Not mandatory by CNAF M-3710.7 but use is encouraged.

## 29
**Q:** Give an example of a SID with higher takeoff weather than the runway’s PAR/ILS.
**A:** Some SIDs require higher takeoff wx (e.g., 300-1) even though the runway has a PAR/ILS needing 200-1/2.

## 30
**Q:** Pilot NAV SID — who navigates and how do you file? Example?
**A:** Pilot primarily navigates; minimum communication; enter SID identifier in DD-1801 route. Example: Kingsville Three with Corpus Christi transition → NQI3 CRP.

## 31
**Q:** Vector SID — who navigates and how do you file? Example?
**A:** ATC provides navigational guidance (hybrid SID/radar); enter identifier in route. Example: Chattanooga Seven Departure (VECTOR) → CHA7 CHA.

## 32
**Q:** When is a SID considered cancelled?
**A:** If established on a SID and subsequently vectored or cleared off the SID transition, SID is cancelled unless the controller adds “expect to resume SID.”

## 33
**Q:** How is a cancelled SID reinstated?
**A:** ATC must indicate the portion of the SID routing that still applies and restate altitude restrictions.

## 34
**Q:** Restated altitude on a SID — may you climb immediately?
**A:** Yes — climb immediately to the restated altitude unless ATC states altitude restrictions remain in effect; still follow the SID path-over-the-ground.

## 35
**Q:** Example DD-1801 SID route string from the lesson?
**A:** CHA7 CHA DCT RMG DCT MGM J39 CEW DCT INBRD DCT JAYDI DCT NPA.

## 36
**Q:** When is a radar departure used?
**A:** When there is no published SID, or you desire to proceed directly to the first point on the route of flight.

## 37
**Q:** How do you file a radar departure on the DD-1801?
**A:** Enter DCT + NAVAID/intersection as the first route entry; Remarks: “REQUEST RADAR DEPARTURE.”

## 38
**Q:** Besides radar departure, what else falls under Specific ATC Departure Instructions?
**A:** Verbal instructions — navigational guidance not based on radar.

## 39
**Q:** What is a VCOA?
**A:** Visual Climb Over Airport — climb in visual conditions to cross the airport or an on-airport NAVAID in a specified direction at/above a specified altitude when climb gradients/terrain preclude a conventional DP. Located in front of DOD approach plates.

## 40
**Q:** How do you file a VCOA?
**A:** VCOAs have no names or identifiers. File your first route point; in REMARKS state that you intend to depart via the VCOA.

## 41
**Q:** VFR Climb on Course — what do you do until IFR pickup?
**A:** Remain below Class A; fly VFR cruising altitudes and cloud clearances until issued an IFR clearance. ATC tells when/where to contact ARTCC.

## 42
**Q:** How do you file VFR Climb on Course? Example RMK?
**A:** Remarks: “REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE” (to where you enter the IFR enroute system). Example: RMK/REQUEST VFR CLIMB ON COURSE.

## 43
**Q:** Example VFR Climb on Course radio call from the lesson?
**A:** “Jacksonville Center, ROKT 21, VFR over Seminole at one zero thousand five hundred, looking to pickup my IFR flight plan to Navy Pensacola.”

## 44
**Q:** What is a STAR?
**A:** Pre-planned IFR arrival providing transition from the enroute structure to an outer fix or IAF.

## 45
**Q:** STAR altitude rule before “descend via”?
**A:** Maintain last assigned altitude until receiving authorization to descend so as to comply with all published/issued restrictions.

## 46
**Q:** What does a “DESCEND VIA” clearance authorize?
**A:** Lateral and vertical navigation in accordance with the depicted procedure to meet published restrictions.

## 47
**Q:** How do you refuse a STAR?
**A:** Indicate in DD-1801 Remarks (preferred), or on initial contact request “No STAR.”

## 48
**Q:** LEMIG ONE ARRIVAL — Cotulla Transition routing?
**A:** From over COT VORTAC via COT R-037 to LEMIG INT; thence… ALL AIRPORTS: from over LEMIG INT via SAT R-175 to ELKAY INT; expect radar vectors to final approach course.

## 49
**Q:** LEMIG ONE — turbojet vertical nav planning note at LEMIG?
**A:** Expect to cross LEMIG at 10,000′.

## 50
**Q:** LEMIG ONE — San Antonio Approach Control frequencies on the chart?
**A:** 125.7 and 381.4.

## 51
**Q:** What is UHF SFA?
**A:** Single Frequency Approach — single UHF frequency throughout approach to touchdown for military single-piloted turbojet aircraft at night or IMC; depicted in IFR Supplement; request from ATC.

## 52
**Q:** CNAF M-3710.7 SFA requirements?
**A:** Single-piloted aircraft on IFR SHALL be provided SFA to the maximum extent communication capabilities and traffic permit. Without SFA, keep frequency and/or transponder code shifts to an absolute minimum below 2500 ft AGL.

## 53
**Q:** Absolute single-piloted fixed-wing approach weather minimums?
**A:** 200 foot ceiling/HAT and 1/2 statute mile visibility / 2400 feet RVR.

## 54
**Q:** T-45 approach minimums rule?
**A:** 200-1/2 or as published, whichever is higher.

## 55
**Q:** Single-piloted straight-in absolute HAT and DA adjustment rule?
**A:** Absolute HAT 200 ft AGL. Adjust DH/DA so lowest point of descent is 200 HAT until you visually acquire the field — add (200 − published HAT) to the approach plate DA.

## 56
**Q:** KNQI S-PAR / HI-ILS example: published DA/HAT and single-piloted adjusted DA?
**A:** Published DA=150, HAT=100. Difference 100 (200−100) added to DA → single-piloted DA=250.

## 57
**Q:** May you commence an approach when dest/alternate weather is below minimums?
**A:** No — you may not commence an actual or practice instrument approach at destination/alternate when weather is reported below approach minimums.

## 58
**Q:** If weather goes below mins after you commence the approach?
**A:** Pilot may, at their discretion, continue the approach to published minimums.

## 59
**Q:** Practice approaches to minimums — where allowed?
**A:** At enroute airfields regardless of weather minimums. Not at filed destination or alternate — must have “no intent” to land.

## 60
**Q:** Formation IMC approach authorization for >2 aircraft?
**A:** Instrument approaches with or without intent to land in IMC by formations of more than two aircraft are not authorized. Penetration of IMC to obtain VMC by >2 aircraft is authorized.

## 61
**Q:** Two-aircraft formation approach weather minimums?
**A:** At least published circling minimums, or 1000-3 if circling approach is not authorized.

## 62
**Q:** Visual approach weather and sighting requirements?
**A:** 1000 & 3 at airport of intended landing; must have airfield OR preceding traffic in sight. If airfield but not preceding traffic, radar separation is provided.

## 63
**Q:** Who may request a visual approach?
**A:** Controller or pilot may request at any time.

## 64
**Q:** Is a visual approach an IAP? MAP? Flight plan effect?
**A:** Not an instrument approach, but an IFR procedure in VMC. No MAP. Acceptance does not cancel the flight plan.

## 65
**Q:** Visual approach cloud clearance note and ATC expectation?
**A:** Cloud clearances not applicable unless required by ops specs (slide refs 500 below / 1000 above / 2000 horizontal). Proceed visually most direct/safe to a normal straight-in final; contact Tower ASAP for go-around/MAP guidance.

## 66
**Q:** Who may request a contact approach, and what visibility is required?
**A:** Only the pilot may request. Reported 1 mile visibility; remain outside clouds; reasonable expectation of continuing to the airfield.

## 67
**Q:** Is a contact approach an IAP? MAP? Sample clearance?
**A:** Yes — it is an IAP and MAP will be provided. Example: “CLEARED CONTACT APPROACH, (at or below XXXX feet), ROUTE (if necessary), IF NOT POSSIBLE (MAP) AND ADVISE.”

## 68
**Q:** Contact approach altitude clearance rule?
**A:** No fixed altitude required, but if necessary cleared “at or below” an altitude at least 1000 ft below any IFR traffic and not below MSA. Radar separation provided.

## 69
**Q:** Circling approach: MDA and maneuvering rules?
**A:** Use circling mins. When runway environment in sight, state intentions and circle by any safe method unless a procedure/restrictions are specified. Do not descend below MDA to remain clear of clouds.

## 70
**Q:** If visual reference is lost while circling — actions?
**A:** Execute MAP for the original approach: immediate climb; initial turn toward the landing runway to stay in obstruction clearance area; continue turn until established on the missed approach course.

## 71
**Q:** When may you leave IAF altitude after radar vectors / DIRECT to IAF?
**A:** Maintain last assigned altitude until cleared for the approach and established on a segment of the published instrument approach; then you may descend. Diagram: Cross IAF → turn shortest direction → FAF.

## 72
**Q:** When is a missed approach conducted, and what must you tell ATC?
**A:** When at MDA and no visual reference exists at the MAP. Mandatory: inform ATC “executing missed approach.”

## 73
**Q:** NORDO missed approach to alternate — steps?
**A:** Squawk 7600; follow published missed approach; proceed to alternate as filed and immediately commence an approach. Consider filing a DRAFT if weather looks bad.

## 74
**Q:** What does filing a DRAFT give you?
**A:** A ROUTE clearance but not an ALTITUDE clearance (you have not received an ATC altitude clearance).

## 75
**Q:** NORDO altitude-to-alternate options if no expected altitude after DRAFT?
**A:** MSA out to 25 nm from each field; or Emergency Safe Altitudes if fields within 200 nm; or 18,000 ft MSL if altimeter ≥29.92, or FL 190 if altimeter <29.92 (any distance).

## 76
**Q:** What is a VDP?
**A:** Advisory defined point on the FAC from which normal descent from MDA to the touchdown area may be commenced with required visual reference. Used on selected nonprecision approaches where MAP would preclude a normal 3° (~300 ft/NM) path.

## 77
**Q:** Dive-and-drive vs constant-angle approach?
**A:** Dive-and-drive: descend to MDA after FAF then level to MAP. Constant-angle: continuous descent through the VDP toward MAP/touchdown.

## 78
**Q:** What is a VDA?
**A:** Visual Descent Angle — descent angle from FAF to the runway threshold at TCH.

## 79
**Q:** What is the VOR MON?
**A:** Minimum Operational Network of VORs providing conventional navigation backup if GPS/GNSS is lost. CONUS coverage at/above 5,000 ft AGL; supports oceanic arrivals and critical military use.

## 80
**Q:** MON airport approach capability and distance note?
**A:** Proceed to a MON airport and fly an ILS or VOR approach without GPS, DME, ADF, or surveillance. A MON airport will rarely be 100 NM distant.

## 81
**Q:** FLIP IFR Enroute Low Altitude — what do BLUE/GREEN vs BROWN airports mean?
**A:** BLUE or GREEN: approved IAP and/or RADAR MINIMA published (FAA TPP or DoD FLIP). BROWN: no published IAP or RADAR MINIMA. Military MON airports have a dedicated symbol row on the legend.

## 82
**Q:** How do you close a flight plan — military vs non-military?
**A:** Navy: verbal close with Tower or Base Ops at military facilities. Non-military: close with flight service by any means available.

## 83
**Q:** Log book “Cover Your Six” — what yearly minimums to track?
**A:** Medical; approaches; instrument/total flight time; types of flights. Maintain progress awareness; check accurate entries and that accomplished flights get logged.
