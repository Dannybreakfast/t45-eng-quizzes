IFR Departure Types
+ Diverse Departure
+ Airfield has at least one instrument approach procedure.
+ No obstacles penetrate the 40:1 slope (obstacle identification surface/OIS).
= Fly runway heading until 400 ft above the field elevation before executing any turns.
+ Maintain a minimum climb gradient of 200 finm (unless a higher one is published) until reaching a minimum IFR
altitude (MEA, MSA, etc.).

===== frame_038.png =====
IFR Departure Types

+ Standard Instrument Departure (SID)

+ Some have minimum climb rates.

+ Some have higher takeoff weather minimums then otherwise would normally prevail (i.e., 300 -1 even though the

runway has a PAR/ILS needing 200 - 1/2).

+ Primary purpose is to provide obstacle clearance protection for aircraft in IMC.
+ Secondary purpose is to increase efficiency and to reduce communications and departure delays.
+ Not mandatory by CNAF M-3710.7 but use is encouraged

What is the VOR “MON™?
The Very high frequency Omni-directional Range (VOR) Minimum Operational Network (MON) provides a conventional
navigation backup service in the event of a loss of Global Positioning System (GPS) signal.
The MON includes the minimum number of geographically situated VORs in the Contiguous United States (CONUS)
necessary to provide coverage at and above 5,000 feet above ground level.
Additionally, the MON supports international oceanic arrival routes and mission critical military use.
