Partial panel flight consists of flying with any type of instrument degradation (HUD, ADI, etc). As a result of the difference in size between the ADI display and standby attitude indicator, altimeter, airspeed, and VSI, your control of the a/c may be erratic. Use the HSI display for heading if available. The standby attitude indicator moves a smaller distance for a given control input so keep this in mind to avoid over controlling. The standby instruments also have a tendency to lag (particularly the standby VSI, the others are good), furthering the possibility of overcontrolling. After a correction has been initiated, time must be allowed for instrument indications to catch up to the a/c’s performance. Do not apply an ever-increasing correction. Do not fixate on one standby instrument. Fight the tendency to eliminate a performance instrument from your scan. You’ll have to scan faster and more intently since the instruments are spread apart. Hawk the standby altitude and don’t use the standby VSI unless stabilized. Your “VSI” to maintain level flight is the standby altimeter and how fast the needle is moving either direction.

Reduce power to idle and extend speed brakes if necessary. Slow to 200 KIAS and note level flight attitude on the ADI display. Select gear down and flaps to half (if required, flaps may be lowered, but not raised, in a turn). The increased lift and drag combination causes a “ballooning” effect that must be countered with firm forward stick force to maintain altitude and prevent climbing. Wait for stick pressure to dampen out before selecting full flaps, if desired. As stick forces settle down, trim nose up as the a/c decelerates. As a/s approaches 160 KIAS, increase power for 150 KIAS (1900 pph for half or 2500 pph for full) (Note: Don’t do this just go right to optimum AOA). Decelerate to optimum AOA (no sooner than 10 nm from runway, 30 deg of FAC) by reducing power, adding noseup trim to maintain level flight, and then adding the appropriate amount of power to achieve level flight.
